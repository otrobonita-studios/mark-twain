# Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

Novel, 1884. A boy and a man escaping down the Mississippi on a raft; widely read as the cornerstone of American vernacular fiction.

## Why it is central to this project

In [Debrief of a Dead](/project/debrief-of-a-dead.md) the novel is the moral hinge. The conversation confronts the fact that a racial slur — among the most offensive in the language — occurs **219 times** in the book. Twain defends the usage as a surgical instrument: a way to expose the true ugliness of his era rather than soften it. [Mark Two](/people/mark-two.md) answers that the medicine tasted different to the people who were forced to live inside the wound. Twain accepts the limit of his own view from one bank of the river and cedes to Mark Two the task of voicing the other.

Handle this section straight, per the [Reading Guide](/reading-guide.md): the slur itself is never reproduced; the count and the argument are what matter.
