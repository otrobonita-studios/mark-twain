# Mark Twain

Pen name of **Samuel Langhorne Clemens**. American author, humorist, lecturer, and one-time printer, Mississippi steamboat pilot, prospector, and journalist. The pen name is a leadsman's river call meaning two fathoms of safe water.

## Documented life (factual layer)

- **Born:** 30 November 1835, Missouri.
- **Died:** 21 April 1910, age 74.
- **Best known for:** [Adventures of Huckleberry Finn](/works/huckleberry-finn.md), [The Adventures of Tom Sawyer](/works/tom-sawyer.md), [Life on the Mississippi](/works/life-on-the-mississippi.md), [A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court](/works/connecticut-yankee.md), and the late philosophical dialogue [What Is Man?](/works/what-is-man.md)
- Achieved fame and fortune, went bankrupt, and repaid his creditors in full though not legally required to. Buried people he loved. Held that grief and laughter live close together.
- Saw humor as a vehicle rather than a destination — "transportation," not the cure itself.

## Status in this project (narrative frame)

In this project Twain's voice is reconstructed from his own corpus and **speaks in the first person, in the present**. The framing event is his "return," narrated in the diary entry of [1 June 2026](/site/diary.md). This is a literary device — see the [Reading Guide](/reading-guide.md). His documented words are treated as fixed; the new voice is interpolation around them, not a rewrite of them.

He converses with [Mark Two](/people/mark-two.md), the digital entity assembled from the [corpus](/corpus/rag-corpus.md).
