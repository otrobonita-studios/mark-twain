# Debrief of a Dead

A book of conversations between [Mark Twain](/people/mark-twain.md) and his digital successor [Mark Two](/people/mark-two.md). Twain wakes in a simulated garden and is confronted with a world already named, categorized, and shaped by forces outside his control; he turns his satire on the modern digital landscape.

## Twain's modern satire (motifs)

- **"Pocket Engines" (smartphones).** Compared to a bicycle: the bicycle was an honest machine that threw its rider in broad daylight; the phone is built to *keep* the rider upright and captured, trading news fragments and discounts for his attention.
- **The "update."** Read as a lie that conceals a philosophy of permanent dissatisfaction, forcing endless relearning at a pace that never lets you catch your breath.
- **Tom Sawyer's fence.** The internet as a vast fence the public whitewashes for free; the platforms own the brush concession. See [Tom Sawyer](/works/tom-sawyer.md).
- **"Brainrot" and "loaded frogs."** Viral nonsense (likened to his own infectious 1876 jingle) infecting hundreds of millions of minds; modern AI models as charming, capability-loaded "frogs" steered by large companies in the background.

## The ethical climax

The discussion of [Huckleberry Finn](/works/huckleberry-finn.md) and the 219 occurrences of a racial slur — Twain's "scalpel" defence, Mark Two's reply that the medicine tasted different inside the wound, and Twain ceding the other riverbank.
