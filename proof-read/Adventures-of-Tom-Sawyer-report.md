## NOTES ON SYSTEMATIC PATTERNS

### 1. Hyphenation of Compound Nouns (Item 6)
The 1876 original hyphenates compound nouns throughout (e.g., "Sunday-school," "church-going," "cave-mouth"). Modern editions often normalize to closed or spaced forms. **Recommendation:** Verify working copy preservation of 1876 hyphenation patterns across a sample of 20-30 instances.

### 2. Spaced Contractions (Item 7)
The 1876 text uses spaced contractions sporadically (e.g., "did n't," "was n't," "don't") in dialogue and some narrative passages. If the working copy has systematically normalized these to modern unspaced forms, this represents silent modernization. **Recommendation:** Spot-check 10-15 contraction instances against original facsimile.

### 3. Dialogue Punctuation & Em-dashes (Item 11)
The 1876 original uses em-dashes and commas inconsistently before dialogue attributions and continuations. This may reflect Twain's compositional practice or compositor practice. Preserve as-is if consistent with original.

### 4. Capitalization of Epithets & Titles (Item 15)
Twain capitalizes character epithets ("Red-Handed," "Robber," "Pirate") when used as appositives in dialogue. Verify this convention is preserved.

### 5. Numeric References (Item 16)
Verify consistency in how numbers are represented: "Number Two" vs. "No. 2" vs. "number two" — original form should be preserved for authenticity.
