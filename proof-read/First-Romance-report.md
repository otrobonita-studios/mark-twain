## Findings

### [Title — "AWFUL, TERRIBLE MEDIEVAL ROMANCE"] ⚠️ PROVENANCE
- **Flag:** Provenance — working copy title is "AWFUL, TERRIBLE MEDIEVAL ROMANCE," which does not correspond to any authoritative title for this piece
- **Source:** The authentic titles are: "1002d Arabian Night" (*Buffalo Express*, Jan. 1870); untitled "First Romance" section (*Sheldon*, 1871); "A Mediaeval Romance" (*Sketches New and Old*, 1875; note period spelling "Mediaeval"). The working copy title is a modern site invention.
- **Suggestion:** Restore to "A Mediaeval Romance" (for the Sketches collected text) or "A Medieval Romance" (modernized spelling); remove "AWFUL, TERRIBLE" entirely
- **Author's choice?** No — Twain never titled this piece "Awful, Terrible Medieval Romance." The site's title appears to be an editorially invented subtitle.
- **Confidence:** High

## Notes on scope

**The working copy text is notably cleaner than the americanliterature.com transcription.** The americanliterature.com version contains multiple OCR errors absent from the working copy: "the great halls of *tie* palace" (for "the palace"), "The old Duke's, heart" (spurious comma), "the love of, the rest" (spurious comma), "He, said" (spurious comma), and "and was going to search the other passengers' baggage, it was too much" where americanliterature.com misreads "his things" as "this things." The working copy has the correct reading in all these cases, suggesting a cleaner source text.

**Title note:** The 1875 collected text uses "Mediaeval" (the period British spelling); most modern reprints use "Medieval." If the working copy aims to represent the 1875 Sketches text, "A Mediaeval Romance" is the appropriate title; "A Medieval Romance" is acceptable for a modernized edition. Either is preferable to the invented "AWFUL, TERRIBLE MEDIEVAL ROMANCE."

**Completeness:** The postscript — "[If Harper's Weekly or the New York Tribune desire to copy these initial chapters..." / "MARK TWAIN"] — is present and correctly placed. The famous self-undermining ending paragraph is present and complete.
