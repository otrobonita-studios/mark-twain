# QUICK REFERENCE GUIDE
## Black Scholarly Positions on *Huckleberry Finn* & Censorship

---

## THE EIGHT KEY VOICES

### **RALPH ELLISON** — The Subversionist
- **Position**: Jim's humanity persists beneath racist form; Twain uses racism against itself
- **On Teaching**: YES. Read carefully.
- **Key Phrase**: "Change the joke and slip the yoke"
- **Your Song Should**: Show that racist forms can be read against racism itself
- **Red Flag**: Don't dismiss the form; engage with what Twain is doing *with* it

### **TONI MORRISON** — The Structural Critic
- **Position**: The book's architecture uses Jim as "Africanist presence" for white consciousness; the problem is deeper than the slur
- **On Teaching**: YES, but unflinchingly. And NO to sanitization (NewSouth Edition is worse)
- **Key Phrase**: "Evasion is worse than confrontation"
- **Your Song Should**: Acknowledge the structural problem, not just the language
- **Red Flag**: Don't pretend changing a word solves anything

### **JOCELYN CHADWICK** — The Pedagogical Defender
- **Position**: Jim is complex; confronting pain builds critical consciousness; students need this literacy
- **On Teaching**: YES. Essential.
- **Key Phrase**: "Pain is educational when properly supported"
- **Your Song Should**: Frame teaching as building critical consciousness, not just "defending the book"
- **Red Flag**: Don't dismiss the harm; acknowledge it, then frame engagement as the solution

### **JULIUS LESTER** — The Instrumentality Critic
- **Position**: Jim is real but instrumentalized; the book is about Huck's education, not Jim's story
- **On Teaching**: PROBABLY NOT. Structure is too compromised.
- **Key Phrase**: "Jim's humanity serves Huck's moral education"
- **Your Song Should**: Engage with WHY Jim's role matters, not just assert his humanity
- **Red Flag**: Don't stop at "Jim is a character"; address the structural use

### **DAVID BRADLEY** — The Unflinching Advocate
- **Position**: The book's power lies in its refusal to sanitize; discomfort is necessary for understanding
- **On Teaching**: YES. Unambiguously.
- **Key Phrase**: "Sanitization hides the problem rather than solving it"
- **Your Song Should**: Frame confrontation as the path to understanding
- **Red Flag**: Don't imply that comfort is an option

### **JOHN H. WALLACE** — The Protection Advocate
- **Position**: The slur's presence in schools harms Black students; removal = boundary-setting, not evasion
- **On Teaching**: NO. Remove from schools. (Not removal from libraries; school curricula specifically)
- **Key Phrase**: "Protection and boundary-setting are acts of care"
- **Your Song Should**: Engage seriously with why classroom harm matters
- **Red Flag**: Don't call his position "censorship" or "evasion"; he's arguing for protection

### **SHARON RUSH** — The Classroom Research Scholar
- **Position**: Documented harm to isolated Black students is real; good intentions don't eliminate it
- **On Teaching**: CONDITIONALLY. Diverse classroom, trained teachers, opt-out options, explicit processing.
- **Key Phrase**: "Classroom conditions determine whether harm is managed or inflicted"
- **Your Song Should**: Acknowledge that context and care matter
- **Red Flag**: Don't dismiss harm as "sensitivity"; it's documented and real

### **PERCIVAL EVERETT** — The Literary Answer
- **Position**: Provide Jim's full narrative authority; answer the deficit with new literature
- **What He Did**: Won the 2024 Pulitzer Prize for *James*, centering Jim as protagonist and narrator
- **Implication**: The solution isn't endless debate about Twain; it's creating narratives Twain couldn't
- **Your Song Should**: If it's 2024+, acknowledge *James* as the new literary fact
- **Red Flag**: Don't treat *Huckleberry Finn* as the only option anymore

## THE POSITIONS MAPPED

| **Stance** | **Who** | **Why** |
|---|---|---|
| **Remove from schools** | Wallace | Student harm is primary concern |
| **Conditional teaching** | Rush, Lester (reluctantly) | Harm is real; conditions can manage it |
| **Teach unflinchingly** | Morrison, Ellison, Chadwick, Bradley | Understanding racism requires confrontation |
| **Literary alternative** | Everett | Write the missing narrative |

## THE CONSENSUS POINTS (Use These)

1. **Everyone opposes the NewSouth Edition (word substitution)**
   - Morrison: "Evasion"
   - Ellison: "Erases the form"
   - Chadwick: "Sanitizes the problem"
   - Lester: "Doesn't address structure"
   - Wallace: "Not what I asked for"
   - Bradley: "Dilutes the truth"

2. **Jim's story matters**
   - No one disagrees that Jim is important

3. **Critical reading is necessary**
   - Can't read the book naively; racism must be engaged

4. **The book has real power**
   - Even critics acknowledge its significance

---

## THE DEEP DISAGREEMENTS (Address These)

**Disagreement 1: Classroom Harm**
- **Wallace/Rush**: Harm is documented and real
- **Chadwick/Bradley**: Pain can be educational with proper support
- **Middle position (Rush)**: Both are true; conditions matter

**Disagreement 2: Removal vs. Engagement**
- **Wallace**: Remove to protect
- **Morrison**: Engage to understand
- **They're addressing different questions**

**Disagreement 3: Jim's Narrative Role**
- **Ellison**: Humanity persists beneath form
- **Lester**: Humanity is instrumentalized
- **Everett**: Humanity requires different narrative

## IF YOU'RE MAKING AN ANTI-CENSORSHIP ARGUMENT

**Your Allies:**
- Morrison (critical but anti-removal)
- Ellison (advocates careful engagement)
- Chadwick (pedagogically pro-teaching)
- Bradley (explicitly anti-censorship)

**Your Real Opponent:**
- Wallace (hardline removal)

**How to Engage:**
- Don't dismiss his concern as "evasion"
- Acknowledge the harm he documents
- Explain why you believe engagement is better than removal
- Be honest that you're asking for something difficult

**Your Strongest Move:**
Use Morrison: "Unflinching reading is harder than removal, but it's necessary."

---

## IF YOU'RE DEFENDING JIM'S HUMANITY

**Don't Just Say**: "Jim is a real character"

**Say Instead:**
- "Jim's humanity persists despite the constraints" (Ellison)
- "Jim is complex and morally capable" (Chadwick)
- "Jim's consciousness matters as much as the boy's" (Opposes Lester)
- "If Jim had his own narrative authority..." (Everett's move)

**What Not to Say:**
- "Jim is fine in the book" (Lester will object immediately)
- "Jim's role in Huck's education is good" (Morrison/Lester will object)

---

## IF YOU'RE ADDRESSING THE SLUR

**Don't Say**: "It's just historical accuracy"
- No scholar accepts this

**Do Say**:
- "The slur shows how racism operated linguistically" (Bradley/Morrison)
- "Confronting the slur unflinching teaches us how it works" (Chadwick)
- "The slur is what makes avoidance impossible" (Ellison)

**Acknowledge**:
- "The slur causes harm to students in mixed classrooms" (Rush/Wallace)
- "But that harm isn't a reason to avoid the text; it's a reason to teach it carefully" (Chadwick)

## IF YOU WANT MAXIMUM AUTHENTICITY

**Research One Scholar Deeply:**
- Read Ellison's essay or Morrison's book or Chadwick's *Jim Dilemma*
- Ground your song in their actual argument
- Your song doesn't have to agree, but it should engage seriously

**Read Everett's *James***:
- This is the new literary fact
- It changes what's possible to say
- Your song is more current if you acknowledge it

**Address the Actual Disagreement**:
- Don't make it a simple "defenders vs. censors" binary
- Show that Black scholars genuinely disagree on the *solution*, not the *problem*
- Most scholars agree the book has a racism problem; they disagree on what to do

## PHRASES THAT WORK

(These engage with actual positions)

- "The book shows how racism works in narrative form" (Morrison)
- "Jim's humanity persists beneath the stereotype" (Ellison)
- "Confronting pain builds critical consciousness" (Chadwick)
- "Sanitizing the text hides the problem" (Bradley/Morrison on NewSouth)
- "A child's safety in their classroom matters" (Rush/Wallace—acknowledge this even if you disagree)
- "We can teach the harm alongside the literature" (Chadwick/Morrison synthesis)

---

## PHRASES THAT DON'T WORK

(These misrepresent positions)

- "Censorship is always wrong" (Ignores Wallace's protection argument)
- "History shouldn't be erased" (History can be taught other ways)
- "People are too sensitive" (Dismisses documented harm)
- "The author didn't intend harm" (Misses structural/architectural argument)
- "Everyone reads it in high school" (Some students can't do this safely)
- "It's just a word" (Shows you haven't engaged with the scholarship)

## THE EVERETT FACTOR (2024+)

**Why This Matters:**
- *James* won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 2024
- It's now the new literary fact in this debate
- It suggests the solution isn't endless critique of Twain but creation of new narratives
- A teacher can now assign *both* books; they complement each other

**What This Changes:**
- Argument shifts from "Should we teach *Huckleberry Finn*?" to "What do students need to read?"
- *James* vindicates critics (Jim's story is that important) without removing Twain
- *James* suggests that understanding Twain's text is *easier* when you've read Jim's own narrative

## FINAL WORD

The Black scholarly community disagrees about *Huckleberry Finn*, but not randomly. The disagreement is structured, philosophical, and rooted in different priorities:

- **Wallace**: Prioritizes student protection
- **Lester/Morrison**: Prioritize understanding structural racism
- **Chadwick/Bradley**: Prioritize critical literacy development
- **Ellison**: Prioritizes careful reading
- **Rush**: Prioritizes conditions and care
- **Everett**: Prioritizes new narratives

You don't have to agree with all of them. But if you engage seriously with *one* of them—really read their argument, understand their reasoning, engage with their evidence—your song will have the depth and authenticity that comes from real scholarship.

That's the only test that matters.
