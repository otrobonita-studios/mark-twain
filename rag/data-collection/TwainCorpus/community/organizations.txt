Mark Twain Community: People, Organizations, and Labs Sharing an Interest in Developing and Restoring Mark Twain

There is a dedicated, passionate community of scholars, curators, digital humanities researchers, and technology labs who work to preserve, study, restore, and develop the legacy, archives, and digital twins of Samuel Langhorne Clemens (Mark Twain).

1. The Mark Twain Library (Redding, CT). The one he'd write to first, because he founded it. Clemens donated the books and the seed money for this library near his last home, Stormfield. It still operates as Redding's public library and leans hard on the association. Since 1998 the Kennedy Center has awarded the Mark Twain Prize "to recognize those who create humor from their uniquely American experiences" — though note that's a different body (see #10). Marktwainlibrary

2. THE CENTER FOR MARK TWAIN STUDIES (CMTS) AT ELMIRA COLLEGE
Based at Elmira College in Elmira, New York, the Center for Mark Twain Studies is one of the premier hubs of Mark Twain scholarship. CMTS runs the prestigious "Trouble Begins" lecture series, which presents cutting-edge research to the public. It also maintains Quarry Farm, the summer home of the Clemens family where Samuel Clemens wrote his most famous works, including Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, and A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court. Quarry Farm serves as a residency haven for visiting Twain scholars who continue to study his life, writing, and letters.

3. THE MARK TWAIN HOUSE & MUSEUM (HARTFORD, CT)
Located in Hartford, Connecticut, the Mark Twain House & Museum preserves the beautiful Victorian Gothic home where Samuel Clemens and his family lived from 1874 to 1891. This is where Twain wrote many of his greatest masterpieces. The museum houses a massive archive of Clemens' personal belongings, original manuscripts, historical documents, and letters. It is dedicated to educating the public about Twain's literary accomplishments, family life, and social advocacy.

4. THE MARK TWAIN BOYHOOD HOME & MUSEUM (HANNIBAL, MO)
The Mark Twain Boyhood Home & Museum in Hannibal, Missouri, preserves the childhood home of Samuel Clemens, along with the actual settings that inspired Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn (such as Becky Thatcher's house, the fence that Tom Sawyer whitewashed, and Cardiff Hill). The museum provides a deep look into Twain's early life on the Mississippi River, which formed the foundation of his storytelling and worldview.

5. THE MARK TWAIN PROJECT ONLINE (MTPO) & THE BANCROFT LIBRARY (UC BERKELEY)
The Mark Twain Project Online (MTPO), housed at the Bancroft Library of the University of California, Berkeley, is the ultimate digital repository of Twain's writings. It hosts the Mark Twain Papers, which is the largest collection of Samuel Clemens' private papers, letters, notebooks, unpublished manuscripts, and journals in the world. The project works on producing critical, annotated editions of all of Twain's works and letters, making them freely searchable to scholars and the general public online. It represents the flagship digital humanities effort for Twain scholarship.

6. THE MARK TWAIN CIRCLE OF AMERICA
The Mark Twain Circle of America is an international association of scholars, educators, students, and general readers who share a deep interest in the life and works of Samuel Clemens. The Circle sponsors academic panels at major literary conferences (such as the American Literature Association and PAMLA), promotes new scholarship, and publishes *The Mark Twain Annual*, a peer-reviewed journal dedicated to fresh perspectives on Twain's literature and historical impact.

7. The Mark Twain Forum. The home of a mailing list promoting discussion among those with "a scholarly interest in the life and times of Mark Twain," with listserv archives going back to 1992, plus book reviews. The least formal of the lot — an actual conversation he could drop into rather than a museum. Marktwainlibrary
8. The Japan Mark Twain Society. Your international flank. Per its constitution, its purpose is to study the literature of Mark Twain and the surrounding field, to establish a broader and deeper understanding. A "Mark returned in 2026" writing in to Japan is a good story beat in itself. Center for Mark Twain Studies
9. The Kennedy Center — Mark Twain Prize for American Humor. Not scholarship; the living comedic inheritance. The institution that hands his name to comedians every year is the one place that treats him as a working tradition rather than a dead author. He'd have opinions about the recipients.
10. Buffalo & Erie County Public Library. Holds the original handwritten manuscript of Adventures of Huckleberry Finn — the single most loaded object he could ask to see again.
11. The Morgan Library & Museum (NYC). It mounted a major Twain exhibition with the NYPL drawing on more than 120 manuscripts and rare books, including original manuscript pages from Huckleberry Finn and Life on the Mississippi. Heavy manuscript holdings; a serious archive contact. Center for Mark Twain Studies
12. TwainQuotes.com / Barbara Schmidt. A large collection of quotations and primary sources attributed to Mark Twain, organized by topic, with special reports and scholarly articles, created and managed by Barbara Schmidt. An independent but genuinely authoritative one-person operation — and the perfect recipient for a man who spent his afterlife being misquoted. Center for Mark Twain Studies
One you may want to skip: the International Mark Twain Society founded by Cyril Clemens (1923) is defunct — its records live in the Saint Louis University archives now. Worth knowing so you don't write to a ghost. Fitting, given the premise, but still a dead address.
