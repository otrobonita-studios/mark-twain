A few times in the past few years, I have felt the need to cite the passage in his memoirs where Isaac Asimov explained why Mensa and other high-IQ societies were not useful places for him to spend his time.

I became uncomfortably aware that Mensans, however high their paper IQ might be, were likely to be as irrational as anyone else. Many of them believed themselves to be part of a “superior” group that ought to rule the world, and despised non-Mensans as inferiors. Naturally, they tended to be right-wing conservatives, and I generally feel terribly out of sympathy with such views.

Isaac Asimov, 
I, Asimov: Memoir
 (Doubleday, 1994) pp. 379-381

Asimov was introverted but he had no shortage of thoughtful friends who were kind people with skills and accomplishments.
