Wikipedia’s founder on his life’s work: “A propaganda tool”

Internet censorship
Published October 1, 2025 – By Editorial staff
Montage. Photo: facsimile/X/Tucker Carlson, Wikipedia
Larry Sanger believes that Wikipedia has been kidnapped and presents nine measures to "save" the encyclopedia.
4 minutes read

Editorial staff editorial@nordictimes.com

Larry Sanger, co-founder of Wikipedia, presents nine radical theses to save his life's work from what he calls ideological kidnapping.

In a comprehensive interview with Tucker Carlson, he reveals that anonymous editors can defame with impunity, that conservative news sources are systematically blocked, and that intelligence services have manipulated the encyclopedia's content.

Larry Sanger, the philosopher who together with Jimmy Wales founded Wikipedia in 2001, paints a grim picture of what has happened to the world's largest encyclopedia. In Tucker Carlson's interview, published on X, he describes how the encyclopedia he created to bring together people with different perspectives has now become a propaganda tool.

— Wikipedia became a weapon of ideological theological war, used to destroy its enemies, Sanger states in the interview.

Tucker Carlson, who himself discovers during the interview that he is described as "a leading voice of white grievance politics" in his Wikipedia article, expresses his frustration over the platform's development.

— I have sent money, like significant money to Wikipedia because I was so thrilled by its existence, Carlson recounts and continues:

— Now it's like the leading source of dishonesty or I would say disinformation. They lie. They leave out key information.

Left early – saw where it was heading

Sanger left Wikipedia as early as 2002, explaining that he saw early signs that Jimmy Wales was allowing "troublemakers and left-wing activists" to take over the platform.

— The left has its march through the institutions. And when Wikipedia appeared, it was one of the institutions that they marched through, Sanger observes.

The original neutrality principle, which Sanger himself formulated, has according to him been distorted into its opposite. Where they previously strived to present all significant perspectives fairly, terms like "minority opinions" and "conspiracy theories" are now used to exclude certain viewpoints.

85 percent anonymous power holders

One of the interview's most astonishing revelations concerns the identity of Wikipedia's editors. According to Sanger's investigation, 85 percent of the 62 most powerful accounts on Wikipedia are anonymous.

— We don't know who they are. They can libel people with impunity, because they're anonymous, says Sanger.

Tucker Carlson compares the situation to if history professors at Harvard and Yale suddenly refused to give their names:

— If all of a sudden every history professor at Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Dartmouth, Cornell, et cetera, decided, I'm not telling you my name, as I teach your kids about the Renaissance or whatever, we would say, that's freaking nuts.

Larry Sanger built Wikipedia as an unbiased repository of the world’s knowledge, and then stood helplessly by as activists and intel agencies turned it into the most comprehensive propaganda op in human history. There’s nothing more corrupt.

Blacklisted news sources

Wikipedia has maintained since 2017 a list of "recurring sources" that in practice functions as an ideological blacklist. During the interview, Carlson goes through the list and discovers that while The New York Times, The Washington Post and CNN get the green light, sources like Fox News, Daily Caller, Breitbart and New York Post are blacklisted.

— You can't cite New York Post on Wikipedia as a factual source, Sanger confirms.

He points out the irony that this blacklist was established the same year Donald Trump took office as president.

Intelligence services' influence

Both Carlson and Sanger discuss the possibility that intelligence services manipulate Wikipedia. Sanger tells about research from 2006-2007 that showed edits made from IP addresses in Langley, Virginia, where the CIA has its headquarters.

— Wikipedia is like just a goldmine for the intelligence agencies of the world because it's like a one-stop shop. You know, you can just like type in the things that you want people to believe, says Sanger.

Nine theses for reformation

Inspired by Martin Luther, Sanger presents nine theses to reform Wikipedia:

  1. Abolish consensus decision-making – The current system allows ideologues to silence dissidents by falsely claiming unanimity.
  2. Enable competing articles – Allow multiple articles on the same topic written from different perspectives.
  3. Abolish source blacklists – The ideologically one-sided blacklist must be removed.
  4. Revive the original neutrality principle – Wikipedia must return to genuine neutrality.
  5. Repeal the "ignore all rules" rule – This rule has been abused to protect insiders from accountability.
  6. Expose Wikipedia's leaders – The anonymous power holders must be publicly identified.
  7. Let the public rate articles – Implement a system where readers can evaluate articles.
  8. End permanent blocking – The practice of permanently blocking accounts is unfair and ideologically motivated.
  9. Adopt a legislative process – Wikipedia needs an elected council to implement reforms.

"It's about our civilization"

Tucker Carlson concludes the interview by emphasizing the importance of Sanger's reform proposals:

— It has to do with history and the collective memory, which is another way of saying you're civilization. It can't exist unless it understands itself.

Sanger, who says he feels personal responsibility for what Wikipedia has become, hopes that his theses will start a broad debate about the platform's future. He has published the proposals on his user page on Wikipedia to take the debate directly to the editor community.

— I hope this starts what Luther started – a return to honesty, says Sanger.

Wikipedia's founding ideals of bringing together the world's knowledge in a neutral way stand, according to Sanger, in sharp contrast to what the platform has become today. The question is whether his reform proposals can reverse the development or whether the encyclopedia, as he puts it, will forever remain "a tool for the ruling class".

Swedish ISP refuses industry pact to expand website blocking

Digital freedom
Published July 10, 2026 – By Editorial staff
Photo: Bahnhof
Server hall and glass-walled meeting space at Bahnhof’s Pionen data center in Stockholm.
1 minute read
  • Copy link
  • Email
  • Facebook
  • LinkedIn
  • Telegram
  • Twitter
Editorial staff editorial@nordictimes.com

Swedish internet provider Bahnhof says it has refused to sign an industry agreement that calls for cooperation on legislation to streamline future website-blocking orders.

The dispute follows two June rulings from Sweden’s Patent and Market Court. In one case, brought by the Premier League, Bahnhof was ordered to block access to websites streaming live sports. Ten days later, the court issued another order covering film-streaming sites following legal action by rights holders including Disney, Netflix, Warner Bros., Paramount, Universal, and Svensk Filmindustri (SF Studios).

Bahnhof says it will comply with the orders because it faces fines if it does not, but it objects to the industry agreement. The 2022 agreement, drafted in both Swedish and English, outlines a simplified process for injunctions against copyright-infringing services. It also states that the parties should cooperate in good faith toward legislation for administrative, or out-of-court, website blocking in Sweden.

Bahnhof says it was the only major Swedish operator not to sign the provisions on new legislation. It has also launched a notification page for users who try to reach a blocked address. The disagreement reflects a broader debate over whether copyright enforcement should rely on court-ordered blocks directed at internet providers or be pursued primarily against the operators of infringing services.

Sweden has permitted copyright-related website-blocking injunctions against internet providers based on earlier court precedents. The new rulings do not by themselves create a general administrative blocking system; the proposed legislative goal cited by Bahnhof is a separate part of the industry agreement.

EU court rules individuals can be prosecuted for sharing RT content

Internet censorship
Published July 7, 2026 – By Editorial staff
Montage. Photo: Luxofluxo/CC BY-SA 4.0; Pavel Byrkin/iStock
2 minutes read
  • Copy link
  • Email
  • Facebook
  • LinkedIn

  • Twitter
Editorial staff editorial@nordictimes.com

The Court of Justice of the European Union has ruled that the ban on Russia Today applies not only to traditional media operators, but also to individuals who make RT videos available on open websites without a profit motive.

The judgment in Case C-67/25, Traugott Ickeroth was issued on July 2 and concerns three people in Germany who are facing criminal charges for repeatedly distributing video clips from RT DE via a publicly accessible website.

According to the court’s press release, the website was free to access and financed only through donations from users. A German court had therefore sought clarification on whether the defendants could nevertheless be regarded as “operators” under the EU’s sanctions rules against RT.

The EU court’s answer is yes. The court held that it is irrelevant whether the distribution takes place in the context of an economic activity.

— The concept of 'operator' covers, in this context, any person who is directly or indirectly responsible for making the prohibited content available, the English version of the press release states.

Broad definition of “operator”

The court also specifies that the assessment does not depend on the scope or duration of the transmission. Nor does the fact that the website is operated without remuneration or with voluntary contributions change the assessment.

The ruling therefore confirms the broad interpretation of the sanctions introduced after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. According to the court, the interpretation is necessary to prevent the dissemination of “propaganda of the Russian Federation” and to protect the Union’s public order and security.

The CJEU does not formally rule on the merits of the German criminal case. The judgment is a preliminary ruling on how EU law is to be interpreted, and the national court will now decide the case in accordance with the EU court’s answer.

Questions over EU commitment to openness

The ruling raises a fundamental censorship debate because the EU simultaneously presents itself as a guarantor of openness, media freedom and free access to information. The ruling establishes that a media ban can also reach individuals who operate open websites without a commercial purpose.

EU Court of Justice rules that the prohibition of broadcasting for Russia Today extends to 3 German private individuals which are subject to criminal charges for publishing, on four occasions, videos form RT in a non commercial site they run.

Critics see this as clear hypocrisy. When the EU speaks of "media freedom" and "information integrity", it often refers to pluralism and resilience against influence operations. But when the same institutions prohibit the distribution of one media company’s content and open the door to criminal consequences for individuals, the line between security policy and censorship becomes harder to draw.

Man fined after calling Merz “lying Fritz”

The threat to freedom of speech
Published June 5, 2026 – By Editorial staff
Photo: Xinhua/Li Hanlin
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz speaks during his summer press conference in Berlin on July 18, 2025.
2 minutes read
  • Copy link
  • Email
  • Facebook
  • LinkedIn
  • Telegram
  • Twitter
Editorial staff editorial@nordictimes.com

A German court has ruled that a Facebook post about Chancellor Friedrich Merz should be prosecuted in the "public interest". The man behind the comment has been fined more than €2,000 after calling Merz "lying Fritz".

The case is one of several in which German authorities have pursued citizens over mocking or critical comments about the German chancellor on social media.

The case stems from a visit Merz made to southwestern Germany in October last year. Ahead of the visit, local police issued a warning about a temporary ban on drone flights. The post prompted a series of angry comments in which Merz was called, among other things, a "lying clown", "Pinocchio" and "lackey", according to German media.

The public prosecutor’s office in Heilbronn told Die Welt that 39 preliminary investigations were opened under Section 188 of the German Criminal Code. The provision covers insults against people in political life if the attacks are deemed capable of substantially impeding their public work. Fifteen of the cases were later dropped due to lack of evidence.

In the case of the phrase "lying Fritz", however, the court reached a different assessment. According to the March decision, the wording could help arouse further negative prejudices or aggression among like-minded people. The fine amounts to more than an average German monthly salary.

Government calls it a normal legal process

A spokesperson for the German government was asked about the cases at a press briefing this week, but declined to comment, citing respect for the judiciary. The spokesperson added that Merz himself had not filed a complaint and described the cases as "normal legal processes" that must be protected.

However, it is difficult to describe state action against citizens over nicknames and political insults directed at the country’s highest-ranking government representative as anything other than a direct interference with political freedom of expression.

Merz under pressure from weak poll numbers

During his time as chancellor, Merz has provoked strong reactions by arguing that the welfare state in its current form is outdated and that Germans should work more instead of taking sick leave.

He has also faced weak poll numbers. According to a recent international survey, Merz has been identified as one of the world’s least popular leaders, and German media have reported that parts of his own party have discussed his future after record-low support.

The issue of freedom of expression in Germany has also been raised by reports that German regulators want to force social media platforms to promote government-approved media in their algorithms. The EU has previously criticized the German government, claiming that hate crime legislation has been misused to restrict freedom of expression.

US to fund free speech initiatives in Europe

Internet censorship
Published February 10, 2026 – By Editorial staff
Montage. Photo: Christian Wiediger, Gage Skidmore/CC BY-SA 2.0
2 minutes read
  • Copy link
  • Email
  • Facebook
  • LinkedIn
  • Telegram
  • Twitter
Editorial staff editorial@nordictimes.com

The Trump administration plans to use government funds to promote free speech in Western democracies. The initiative targets European countries where Washington believes online regulations restrict free debate.

A senior US State Department official announced on Monday that the United States will fund free speech efforts in allied countries. The announcement came during a European visit where Sarah Rogers, Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy, is meeting officials and other stakeholders in Dublin, Budapest, Warsaw, and Munich.

— I want to promote free speech in Western allied democracies, and that's what my grant-making is going to be doing, Rogers said during a panel discussion in Budapest.

US officials have strongly opposed European regulations such as the EU's Digital Services Act (DSA) and the UK's Online Safety Act (OSA). Washington argues that these frameworks stifle free speech, particularly regarding criticism of immigration policy, while imposing burdensome requirements on American tech companies.

Plans to fund allied organizations

According to a Financial Times report last week, Rogers has discussed with representatives of the British opposition party Reform UK the possibility of funding think tanks and charitable organizations that support President Donald Trump's MAGA policies.

A State Department spokesperson did not confirm the specific funding, but described the plan as "a transparent, lawful use of resources to advance US interests and values abroad".

In January, the US issued visa bans against a former EU Commissioner and four individuals working against "disinformation", whom Washington accuses of having censored American social media platforms. European leaders condemned the bans and defended Europe's right to legislate how foreign companies operate locally.

The Trump administration's national security strategy from December accused European leaders of censoring free speech and suppressing opposition to immigration policy.
