Why is Anthropic's public writing style so unlike Claude's?

Aug 3, 2026

Claude has a very distinctive writing style: short punchy sentences and eyeball kicks. We have 
automatic highlighting tools
 that don’t even try to predict LLM use in a fancy statistical way, only match on phrase patterns that hardly anyone used prior to Claude, but which now appear everywhere thanks to Claude.

Most of the time, I don’t even need the highlighting tool. I’ll start reading something 
like this
, 
or this
, and think 
this is obviously Claude’s voice
.

“Menu layout? Nearly the same. You’re not relearning a browser — you’re just using a cleaner version of what you already know.”

“K3 ships zero draft tensors — no MTP, no EAGLE — so the only speculative path is an external block-diffusion draft: RadixArk’s Kimi-K3-DSpark.”

In addition to Claude itself sounding like this, so do LLM that are even mildly distilled from Claude (such as Kimi K3). Maybe it’s also 
people
 who are exposed to enough Claude output to unintentionally pick up the style in their own writing. 😱

LLM output doesn’t 
have
 to sound like this! GPT-5 and Gemma 4 write more ’normally’. So, Claude’s voice is at least a semi-deliberate choice by Anthropic. It doesn’t appear to be 
Amanda Askell
’s fault.

Maybe it has emerged from a few million 
RLHF
 nudges where someone picked sentence A as sounding better than B? Has Claude gradient-descended onto the style that people actually prefer in aggregate? I’m not sure I would buy that. The marketplace of ideas has long rewarded authors for using whatever style readers find compelling, and these incentives playing out over centuries did not tilt the total written record in a Claudey direction.

Whatever the case, here is what I find most interesting. 
Anthropic’s public writing does not use the Claude voice, not even a little bit!
 It’s articulate but easy-reading, almost conversational prose, like a seasoned blogger. Go read about the 
J-Space
 or skim 
Claude’s Constitution
. If Claude helped write these at all, they did a great job of hiding it.

We know that Anthropic 
dogfoods
 Claude extensively. 
Claude now writes 80% of Anthropic’s code
, so why not for prose? Why does Anthropic voice their research writeups and policy positions 
so differently
 than they voice their product?

Some conjectures, ordered from mundane to wildly speculative.

We know Dario is an avid essayist. Maybe he prefers to continue writing in his own voice for a public audience, no matter what the LLMs sound like. I can relate!

Anthropic’s brand trust (such as it is these days) is built on ’the humans are in control’. It’s bad optics to create the appearance of the LLM having even a little influence over the most important things that your company says. Thus, the research and PR pieces should not sound like the product, probably not ever.

There is a constituency within Anthropic, having enough influence over Claude’s voice, who believe that, for a variety of reasons rhyming with safety and transparency and noticing ‘adversarial’ distillation, Claude’s writing should be distinct and detectable to observant humans. So, they pepper the output with idiolectic markers that don’t show up in 
every
 sentence, but in enough sentences that a few paragraphs of Claude text are unmistakable. Separately within Anthropic, the people in charge of copy editing the research and policy essays find these same idiolectic markers exhausting and cliché, so they actively remove them. (Or, maybe they have access to an internal-only model with a different voice.)

If you agree that Claude’s voice makes it less useful for writing to sufficiently-discerning audiences, it seems that that the market will push Anthropic to ’tune it out’ over time. Maybe this is already happening if Fable sounds incrementally less Claude-y than prior models, but Opus 5 sure has it.

What gives?

I’m not multi-lingual enough to answer this, but now I’m wondering: how Claude-y does Claude sound in non-English languages?
