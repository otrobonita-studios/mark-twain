OSM Tag History

StyleInfo

The 
boundary
=
administrative
 tag is used on 
relations that define an administrative area
 − territories recognised by governments for administrative purposes. This works rather like 
multipolygon
 – the relation consists of one or more boundary ways that must make up a closed 
area
. 

Administrative boundaries range from large groups of nation-states down to small administrative districts. An indication of their size/level of importance is given by the tag 
admin_level
=*
 which takes a value from 1 to 11, as described below. Many administrative boundaries are currently also tagged with 
place
=*
.

It is recommended that all boundary areas are represented by 
type
=
boundary
 relations. Their border ways may be left completely untagged, with only 
 
outer
 and 
 
inner
 defined as appropriate. 
boundary
=
administrative
 is also often duplicated on the ways that build the relation, but such tagging is optional and may be safely skipped
[
1
]

Locating the administrative boundary

When mapping administrative boundaries, beware of the special cases mentioned in this section.

Administrative boundary in case of disputes

Main article:
 
Disputed territories

With regard to boundary conflicts, please note:

OSM follows 
On the Ground Rule
. Boundaries recorded in OpenStreetMap are the ones that are the most widely internationally recognised and that best meet realities on the ground, generally meaning physical control. In areas without clearly defined borders, the line is approximate
[
2
]
.

It does not mean that OSM community considers specific invasions/wars/whatever that resulted in border change as good/desirable/welcome/wrong. OSM community attempts to map world as it exists, not as it should exist.

In some cases borders may overlap without making it an incorrect tagging. It may be result of agreement between countries to administer area together
[
3
]
 or to mark areas not controlled by any country, with both claiming it.
[
4
]
 In some countries, subnational boundaries overlap in complex ways in reality, so data consumers and quality assurance tools should not assume that an administrative boundary is a 
subarea
 of only one administrative boundary.
[
5
]

Administrative boundary at sea

Main article:
 
Tag:boundary=maritime

Administrative boundaries should only include:

land;

internal waters, and;

territorial sea (often, but not always, 12 nautical miles wide).

Administrative boundary relations should never contain international waters. See 
boundary
=
maritime
 for how to map maritime zones of coastal states.

Whether territorial sea and internal waters are included in subnational administrative divisions, varies per country.

Administrative boundary formed by a river or road

Sometimes borders and rivers/roads (or other linear objects) are tagged together on one object or their nodes are 
glued
 together. It is often undesirable and problematic, as a user modifying a river or road after its geometry changes may easily also modify the border.

A border should be glued with other elements only if they are defined in such a way that if the river changes its course, the element will also change. For example, if an administrative boundary is defined by the 
current
 course of a river, then gluing would be OK. But it is really rare, and usually river-defined borders are defined by something like "the course of a river in the year 1995". This would mean that if the river's shape changed, the shape of the border would not. Moreover, gluing a boundary to a 
waterway
=
river
 way implies that the boundary is defined by the river's 
thalweg
 (or whatever channel the way follows), which is 
sometimes incorrect
.

Avoiding accidental modifications

In addition to avoiding "gluing" boundaries to other elements, accidental modifications to boundaries can be further reduced through filters in editing programs.

iD

To hide boundaries in the iD editor, select the 'Map Data' icon (or use the shortcut 'U') to bring up the Map Data side-menu. Then, from the Map Features section in the side-menu, deselect 'Boundaries'.

JOSM

To hide boundaries in JOSM, refer to 
the official help documentation
.

marks country borders, such as the border between Austria and Germany. The names of the countries are specified through the tags 
left:country
=*

right:country
=*
, if used on a linear way rather than an enclosed/area way.

There is some controversy about what is the 
exact
 meaning of country. For the sake of clarity, only political entities listed on the 
ISO-3166-1 standard
 with the property "Independent=Yes" are to be considered countries. As of September 30, 2025, there are four accepted exceptions:

Kosovo

Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic

Taiwan

State of Palestine

There are polities currently tagged with admin_level=2 which are not independent (not United Nations members, cannot sign international agreements). Most have a special status of a "territory/dependency" of another country. It has been argued that they should be tagged with admin_level=3 or admin_level=4 to be consistent with other countries (like 
Puerto Rico
, a non-state territory of USA with admin_level=4, or Hong Kong, special territory of China with admin_level=3). Do not make such a change without discussing it with the relevant communities!

Please don't add new country with the tag 
admin_level
=
2
. If you think a country could be added, please open a talk and try to have the greatest possible consensus before creating it.

Country specific values of the key 
admin_level
=*

When using admin_levels please refer to and apply the correct 
admin_level
=*
 tag based on the values in the table below.

All countries are documented but some need to be improved :

if the country has a "proposed" clue, it means that the levels are not properly defined. The best way to solve that is to open a talk with mappers of the country to (try to) find an agreement about the hierarchy of administrative boundaries. You can also use the 
tool
 to see if the proposed levels at least fit with the use.

if the country has an "in use" clue or nothing, the meaning is not clear because never defined. We suggest to change/improve this clue.

if the country has an "agreed" clue, it means that there was a talk between mappers and an agreement has been reached (a good practice would be to add a link to that talk).

Tool

You can use this overpass query 
https://overpass-turbo.eu/s/1J4N
 to get:

all nodes, ways and relations of a specific country which describe a government administration unit and include the admin_level tag. Just replace, on the third line, "Afghanistan" by the appropriate name (in english) of the country that you want to control

in the result, for each line, you will have the type of the element (node, way, relation), the id of the element and several tags of the element = admin_level, name, name:en, wikidata, wikipedia
