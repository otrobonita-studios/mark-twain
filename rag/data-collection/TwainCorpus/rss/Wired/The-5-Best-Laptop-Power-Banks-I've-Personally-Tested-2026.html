Laptop Power Bank FAQ

What Features Should I Look for in a Power Bank?

There are a few things worth thinking about when you’re shopping for a portable charger.

The capacity of a power bank is measured in milliampere-hours (mAh), but this can be a little misleading because the amount of power you get out depends on the cable you use, the device you're charging, and the charging method (Qi wireless charging is less efficient). You will never get the maximum capacity. We try to provide a minimum estimate of what you’ll get in terms of charges for a laptop battery.

The charging rate for devices like smartphones is measured in watts (W), but most power banks list the voltage (V) and the amperage (A). Thankfully, you can calculate the wattage yourself simply by multiplying the voltage and amperage. Unfortunately, getting that maximum rate also depends on your device, the standards it supports, and the charging cable you use. For best results match the charging speed across the power bank, cable, and laptop.

Your device will need pass-through support if you want to charge your power bank and use it to charge another device simultaneously. Many portable chargers support pass-through charging. Anker discontinued support for pass-through in some of its products because it found that differences between the output of the wall charger and the input of the device being charged can cause the power bank to cycle on and off rapidly and shorten its life. We would advise caution when using pass-through, as it can also cause portable chargers to heat up.

Can I Take a Power Bank on a Flight?

It’s safe to fly with a power bank, but there are two main restrictions to keep in mind when you board a flight: You must have the portable charger in your carry-on luggage (it cannot be checked), and it must not exceed 100 watt-hours (WH). If your power bank has a capacity larger than 27,000 mAh, you should check with the airline. Below that should not be a problem.

Some airlines have introduced stricter rules, so always check with your chosen airline before you travel. For example, some airlines prefer you not to use or charge power banks during a flight, some require power banks to be in your hand luggage under the seat in front of you and not in the overheads, and some limit the number of power banks you can have onboard.

How I Test Power Banks

I've been testing power banks for close to two decades, and my home office has stacks of ’em at any given moment. I test every portable charger that comes in for at least two weeks. I always check how many times it can fully charge my test laptop (currently a MacBook Air). I'll also try charging my Pixel 10 Pro XL, iPhone 16, and whatever else I have in. For this guide, I also tested with a Chromebook. I test how long it takes to recharge each empty power bank.

Aside from standard tests, I try to use the power banks in my everyday life, just to see how they feel, and my wife and kids often help out and share their opinions. For some of our top picks, I continue to test over months to get a sense of their reliability and durability, though it's not possible to do this for every device that lands on my desk. Beyond function, I take form and usability into account. I keep an eye on user reviews and consider the warranties and customer service of each manufacturer.
