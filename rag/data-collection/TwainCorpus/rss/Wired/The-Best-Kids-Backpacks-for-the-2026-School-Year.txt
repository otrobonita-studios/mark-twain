As an adult
 gear tester, I have one major problem with looking for the best kids’ backpacks: Ultimately, the decision is not up to me. My 8-year-old and 10-year-old are the ones who have to carry their backpacks to school, camp, and beyond. The bag has to protect iPads and Chromebooks; fit lunch boxes and water bottles; and endure getting kicked around cars, bikes, and school gyms. Most importantly, it has to be comfortable. Less important but still crucial, it has to be a good fashion accessory. 
And
 you have to be able to find it quickly when you hang it on a hook next to 50 virtually identical backpacks.
To find the best kids’ backpacks, children of WIRED’s Reviews team tested the following for weeks, if not years, by stuffing them into lockers and under plane seats. Don’t forget to check out the rest of our parenting coverage, which includes the 
Best Laptops for College Students

My parents bought me a new JanSport backpack every fall, but I’ve taken a different approach with my daughter. She got three years out of her 
pint-sized Deuter
 and is now entering her second year with this L.L.Bean pack, in her favorite shade of teal, which is embroidered with her name.
This is a large pack, able to hold more heavy stuff than a fourth grader has any business carrying. It’s got a mesh side pocket for a water bottle and a couple of flat zippered pockets to hold things you want to separate from the main compartment. What we both like about it (other than the embroidered name) is that it’s incredibly sturdy without looking overbuilt. After a trip through the washer to clean out the past year’s spilled yogurt and stains from capless markers, it’s basically back to its original condition.

State Bags (Double Pocket)
When my son found out that my daughter was going to get her own tester State backpack to match her 
State carry-on suitcase
, he immediately demanded his own. State is known for its fantastical matching patterns that extend from luggage and backpacks to 
lunch bags ($48)
. Its products are pricey, but the kid bags are not worse in quality or fewer in features than the adult bags, which I appreciate. It makes sense, considering kids are often harder on their bags than adults are on theirs.
The Kane kids backpacks come in several sizes. Now that my kids are 8 and 10 years old, they each have the largest double-pocket backpack, which has two separate compartments so that they can separate their dirty clothes or muddy shoes from delicate electronics. Its 23.6-liter capacity can fit up to a 15-inch laptop and is more than capacious enough for a Chromebook, sweatshirt, 
Omie lunch box
, and 
mini Nalgene water bottle
. In fact, these items don’t even use all of the space; my kids probably could’ve stuck with the medium-sized backpack for a little while longer.
On a weekend trip, my son stuffed several big books, a Nintendo Switch, and a full fleece blanket into his. There are also two water bottle pockets, a luggage pass-through so you can attach it to the adjustable handle of a matching carry-on, and a hidden AirTag pocket, so you can keep an eye on them in the airport.

GoRuck
I know what you’re thinking, a GoRuck for kids—how much is that going to set me back? Version 2.0 of GoRuc’s KR1 kids pack is actually not that expensive at $65. Yes, that’s more than some on this page, but this is also an extremely tough backpack made of 1000D cordura (except the part against the back, which 200D to be easier on clothes) just like the 
GoRuck packs for adults
. My son has had version one of the KR1 for almost four years now and it looks like it did the day he got it. Version two looks even better, with more fun colors and a larger, easier to access kangaroo pocket. There’s a good balance of organization versus one big compartment here, with a front pocket, two side water bottle pockets, and an internal pocket that’s big enough for pencils, pens, and other small items.
As with any GoRuck, the Shoulder straps are extra padded and won’t cut into your kids’ shoulders when they load it up with books. There’s an internal sleeve to separate out a tablet, but the space there is a little small for a laptop. The top loop handle is sturdy and makes it easy to carry it around without throwing it over a shoulder, and there’s plenty of velcro for patches if your kid is into those. “Kids pack” is a vague term, but I’d say for most this will do well up to about the 10- to 11-year-old age range at which point it’ll likely be a bit small.

Kindred Bravely
My son is headed into transitional kindergarten this fall, and after years at a day care that only allowed bedding in a labeled pillowcase, it’s finally time for his first backpack. I wanted something that would feel timeless so that he could wear it for as many years as possible. My son also isn’t really into the classic characters available on a lot of kids’ backpacks. Storq’s new line immediately caught my eye for the colorful designs that feel fun for kids and easy to recognize as their own. My son is excited to wear the navy-and-green striped option.
Storq, a subsidiary of the Kindred Bravely maternity brand, launched an entire collection for back-to-school gear for younger kids, including the Cool Kid backpack. The Cool Kid has a nice large front pouch, two side pockets for water bottles (they’re slim but easily fit my son’s 16-inch Owala water bottle), and a main zippered pouch with one large pocket plus a spot for their name. You can also personalize the backpack with your kid’s name for an extra $15 when ordering. The backpack uses heavyweight nylon that you can wipe clean.

REI
My 8-year-old loves this backpack, which he has been using for camp all summer. That’s not surprising, as this bag is optimized for hiking, not for carrying standard-sized folders to school. It fits better than most of the other backpacks here, being narrow enough to sit comfortably between his shoulders and short enough to fit his back. It has a chest strap and a waist belt so that it doesn't swing wildly around when he starts sprinting randomly around. The waist belt even has a pocket big enough to fit a Reese’s Peanut Butter Cup.
The pack’s 18 liters isn't quite big enough for a weekend trip, but it’s plenty big for a sweatshirt, Omie lunch box, and Nalgene for camp. There are six exterior pockets (which are all inexplicably filled with rocks right now) and a mesh back panel to keep his back cool. He can also crucially manage all the buckles and adjust the straps himself. The shell is DWR-treated to repel water, but it’s still best to not throw it into a lake with an iPad inside.

FAQs
How do you size kids’ backpacks?
Just like an adult, a child can comfortably carry a pack that is no broader than their shoulders and no longer than their torso. Also, the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that no kid carry a backpack that's more than 
15 percent of their body weight
.
We do not live in a fantasyland, however, and kids regularly have to carry insanely heavy backpacks, so my guideline is to check your school’s requirements about backpacks to make sure yours complies. Then have your kid put on the bag and see if it dangles to their knees, and see if a folder fits inside. Don’t forget to 
adjust the straps
!
How can I make the bag last the whole school year?
It is incredibly annoying to have a backpack break midyear, which is why we only recommend bags that we’ve thoroughly tested ourselves. We also gave more consideration to brands like L.L.Bean, which offer 
good warranties
 for replacements or repairs.
