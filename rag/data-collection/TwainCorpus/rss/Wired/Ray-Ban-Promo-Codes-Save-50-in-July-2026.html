I still remember my first pair of Ray-Bans: the Original Wayfarer Classics with polished black frames and green lenses. Then, in middle school, when Aviators were all the rage, I convinced my mom to buy me a pair with gold metal frames and orange flash polarized lenses. Looking back, they were a bold choice for a 13-year-old girl, but I wore them everywhere. By my freshman year of college, I saved up for a pair of Round Metal sunglasses with rose gold frames and blue gradient lenses. They were the first sunglasses I ever bought with my own money, and more than a decade later, I still have them. Mind you, the lenses are covered in scratches, but I can’t bring myself to part with them.

For many people, Ray-Bans are the first sunglasses they ever owned, or the pair they keep coming back to. They’re the reliable classics that have stood the test of time, aging gracefully while staying relevant across generations. From kids and teens to adults, Ray-Bans have a universal appeal, offering iconic designs and quality at a more approachable price point than many luxury eyewear brands.

And Ray-Ban isn’t just coasting on its heritage.
