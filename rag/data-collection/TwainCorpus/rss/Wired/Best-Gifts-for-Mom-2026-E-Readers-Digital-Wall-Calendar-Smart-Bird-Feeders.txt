The style of espresso made by a superautomatic machine like this Rivelia is generally a bit smoother and less intense than the sort you get in a café. For most moms, showers are sacred—
especially
 for moms of babies or younger kids. However, heavy metals, sediments, chlorine, and PFAS can cause health problems 
not just in drinking water
, but in water that touches the skin, as well. They can also cause buildup on hair and shower surfaces, making them harder to clean. Many municipalities—including mine—treat water with chlorine, and when I asked him which filter he'd recommend for my own shower, WIRED 
water filter reviewer Matthew Korfhage suggested
 the Weddell Duo. It's the only US filter he's found that has 
certification
 for chlorine and lead removal from NSF International, and it also cites third-party testing by 
IAPMO R&T
 showing it can remove PFAS, though there's no corresponding certification. Still, the Duo has a 2.5-gallon-per-minute flow rate and can remove most chlorine from over 8,000 gallons' worth of showers. It's an in-line filter, so it's easy to install in minutes—just screw it in between your pipe and showerhead. It even comes with a little roll of waterproof tape to ensure a leak-free fit, and because of the horizontal orientation, installation won't affect the showerhead height. The filter needs to be replaced every three months, but refills only run 
about $25
, compared with competitors like Filterbaby or Rorra, which can cost $40 to $45.
