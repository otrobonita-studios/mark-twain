When I was first hired as a commerce editor at WIRED in summer 2024, one of the first media requests I fielded was from a nationally renowned talk show. The ask was for an editor to speak on the popularity of portable and neck fans. High-temperature records were breaking all over the US, and abroad, neck fans were suddenly the “it” accessory at the Paris Olympics, hugging the nape of athletes like Simone Biles. “I can't do this,” I told my manager at the time. “I've never even worn a neck fan! I don't have an opinion!”

How times have changed. Two years later, curiosity has gotten the best of me, and I have tried both neck and handheld fans. I can say with confidence that, once you've gotten used to having a little fan with you everywhere you go, enduring high temperatures without one will henceforth feel unbearable. I now own an entire bin full of neck fans, and half of my office is now overtaken by boxes of portable fans, misting fans, and all manner of inexpensive, portable cooling devices. And now I have an opinion. Many, in fact.

An Amazon search for “portable fan” will yield more than 60,000 results, many of which feature weird names and AI art, dubious battery-life claims, and wildly varying features and build quality. Even if most of these portable fans are just $20, no one wants to waste a Jackson on an item that's going to fall apart after one season or stop misting halfway through a vacation.

I spent all summer (and in some cases, last summer, too) testing the fans I recommend below. Be sure to check back periodically, as I’ll update this guide regularly with new products and observations.

How I Tested

I (or another WIRED reviewer) tested each of these fans in real life. The fans came to weddings, festivals, and tournaments; they went on vacations; and they sat on desks to gauge if the noise level was distracting or livable. I tested all available features and attachments, used a decibel meter and anemometer to measure noise and wind speed, practiced holding a conversation while running them, and transported them in a bag to see if they were awkward or easy to carry.

Why Does Charge Capacity Matter?

Charge capacity is the total amount of electricity—and cooling power—that rechargeable battery-powered fans can store and deploy. Measured in milliampere-hours (mAh), or one-thousandth of an ampere-hour (the ability to provide one amp of power for one hour), the higher a fan's charge capacity number, the longer it can go between charges. How this measure translates to run time heavily depends on speed, fan size, and features used. The measure also varies widely between fans.

How I Obtained the Fans

Some fans were samples I obtained from the fan companies with no promise or guarantee of coverage; other fans I purchased and expensed. After testing, I keep the fans on hand for future testing, noting any malfunctions.
