As an adult gear tester, I have one major problem with looking for the best kids’ backpacks: Ultimately, the decision is not up to me. My 8-year-old and 10-year-old are the ones who have to carry their backpacks to school, camp, and beyond. The bag has to protect iPads and Chromebooks; fit lunch boxes and water bottles; and endure getting kicked around cars, bikes, and school gyms. Most importantly, it has to be comfortable. Less important but still crucial, it has to be a good fashion accessory. And you have to be able to find it quickly when you hang it on a hook next to 50 virtually identical backpacks.

To find the best kids’ backpacks, children of WIRED’s Reviews team tested the following for weeks, if not years, by stuffing them into lockers and under plane seats. Don’t forget to check out the rest of our parenting coverage, which includes the Best Laptops for College Students, the Best Paper Planners, and the Best Subscription Boxes for Kids.

Updated August 2026: I’ve added new backpacks from The North Face, Simple Modern, Kindred Bravely, and Go Ruck, and ensured up-to-date links and prices.

    When my son found out that my daughter was going to get her own tester State backpack to match her State carry-on suitcase, he immediately demanded his own. State is known for its fantastical matching patterns that extend from luggage and backpacks to lunch bags ($48). Its products are pricey, but the kid bags are not worse in quality or fewer in features than the adult bags, which I appreciate.     The Kane kids backpacks come in several sizes. Now that my kids are 8 and 10 years old, they each have the largest double-pocket backpack, which has two separate compartments so that they can separate their dirty clothes or muddy shoes from delicate electronics. Its 23.6-liter capacity can fit up to a 15-inch laptop and is more than capacious enough for a Chromebook, sweatshirt, Omie lunch box, and mini Nalgene water bottle. In fact, these items don’t even use all of the space; my kids probably could’ve stuck with the medium-sized backpack for a little while longer.

    On a weekend trip, my son stuffed several big books, a Nintendo Switch, and a full fleece blanket into his. There are also two water bottle pockets, a luggage pass-through so you can attach it to the adjustable handle of a matching carry-on, and a hidden AirTag pocket, so you can keep an eye on them in the airport.

    I know what you’re thinking, a GoRuck for kids—how much is that going to set me back? Version 2.0 of GoRuc’s KR1 kids pack is actually not that expensive at $65. Yes, that’s more than some on this page, but this is also an extremely tough backpack made of 1000D cordura (except the part against the back, which 200D to be easier on clothes) just like the GoRuck packs for adults. The top loop handle is sturdy and makes it easy to carry it around without throwing it over a shoulder, and there’s plenty of velcro for patches if your kid is into those. “Kids pack” is a vague term, but I’d say for most this will do well up to about the 10- to 11-year-old age range at which point it’ll likely be a bit small. —Scott Gilbertson

    Tarn 18

    My 8-year-old loves this backpack, which he has been using for camp all summer. That’s not surprising, as this bag is optimized for hiking, not for carrying standard-sized folders to school. It fits better than most of the other backpacks here, being narrow enough to sit comfortably between his shoulders and short enough to fit his back. He can also crucially manage all the buckles and adjust the straps himself. The shell is DWR-treated to repel water, but it’s still best to not throw it into a lake with an iPad inside.

FAQs

How do you size kids’ backpacks?

Just like an adult, a child can comfortably carry a pack that is no broader than their shoulders and no longer than their torso. Also, the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that no kid carry a backpack that's more than 15 percent of their body weight.

We do not live in a fantasyland, however, and kids regularly have to carry insanely heavy backpacks, so my guideline is to check your school’s requirements about backpacks to make sure yours complies. Then have your kid put on the bag and see if it dangles to their knees, and see if a folder fits inside. Don’t forget to adjust the straps!

How can I make the bag last the whole school year?

It is incredibly annoying to have a backpack break midyear, which is why we only recommend bags that we’ve thoroughly tested ourselves. We also gave more consideration to brands like L.L.Bean, which offer good warranties for replacements or repairs.
