 years since Apple removed the headphone jack from the iPhone. Yes, you can get

What Happened to the Headphone Jack?
AccordionItemContainerButton
It all began when Apple 
removed the port
 on the 
iPhone 7
 in 2016—yes, technically Oppo did it first, but Apple's decision sparked an industry trend. Apple famously cited space-saving reasons to make the phone slimmer and fit a bigger battery, but it was also to nudge along the burgeoning 
wireless headphone
 market. (Guess who 
sits at the top right now
?) Since then, other companies followed swiftly until the port was found only on budget and midrange phones.
Now, 
cheap phones
 are beginning to lose the port, likely because you can buy decent wireless earbuds for 
as little as $30 these days
. The 3.5-mm port will likely disappear entirely from all smartphones within the next few years.
Does Plugging In Improve Audio Quality?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Yes, plugging in a pair of wired headphones means the audio is not compressed, so it can transmit more detail, offering a higher-fidelity audio experience. There's also little or no latency, meaning there's no delay between the music coming from your phone and the audio playing through the headphones. Wired connections also offer zero connectivity issues, and you won't have to worry about battery life. Some wired earbuds also have physical buttons for music playback controls, which always beats out the finicky touch controls on most earbuds.
On the other hand, today's Bluetooth codecs are significantly better than those from even five years ago. Most people will not hear a major difference in audio quality between wired and wireless headphones.
You Can Use a USB-C Dongle
AccordionItemContainerButton
Not having a 3.5-mm port doesn't mean you can't use wired headphones with a smartphone. You can buy a 
USB-C headphone dongle
 that plugs into your smartphone's USB-C port and adds a 3.5-mm port.
Apple USB-C to 3.5-mm Adapter for $9
.
 This adapter from Apple will work with any smartphone with a USB-C port—that means Android phones and the iPhone 15 and newer. I recommend buying a few because they’re easy to lose. If you have an iPhone with a Lightning port (iPhone 14 or earlier), you'll need to use a third-party dongle, as Apple has officially discontinued its adapter.
Google USB-C Earbuds for $31
:
 Alternatively, you can use USB-C earbuds. They're standard wired earbuds, but instead of a 3.5-mm connector at the end, there is a USB-C connector that plugs into your phone's charging port. I've used these Google earbuds for a few years—they sound decent and are cheap. You can probably find plenty of other USB-C headphones, but make sure you check reviews to see what's worth your money.
What Happened to Asus Phones?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Asus was one of the few companies churning out flagship smartphones with the headphone jack. Sadly, the company 
confirmed earlier this year
 that it is exiting the mobile business altogether—say goodbye to the Zenfone and ROG Phone series. It'll continue supporting the phones as promised, but there are no new device launches for 2026. The company claims it's a “temporary” pause, so there's always a chance it could return to the market.

If a headphone jack is a must-have on your next phone, Motorola's 
Moto G Stylus 5G 2026
 is hands-down your best option. Not only does it have the jack, but it also stores a stylus inside, an embarrassment for any other company that cited space-saving reasons to nix the port. The problem is that Motorola didn't add much new in the 2026 iteration, yet it upped the price—it even has the same processor as the 2025 model but 
less
 storage to start. That's par for the course with 
most electronics these days
.
Performance is completely fine, thankfully, and you get a 
120-Hz
, 6.7-inch OLED screen. It's slim, despite the 5,200-mAh battery, which can last two full days with light to average use. The 50-megapixel main camera is reliable day or night as long as you keep still when tapping the shutter button and your subject isn't moving; otherwise, you'll get a lot of blurry images.
Even if it now starts with 128 GB of storage, there's a microSD card slot to expand space, something you won't find on most smartphones these days. It also sports wireless charging and an 
IP68/69 rating
 so it will survive an accidental drop in the pool. Motorola's offering two Android OS upgrades and 3 years of security updates, which is not great considering the price of this phone—the 
Google Pixel 10a
, for example, will receive seven years of blanket updates.
At $500, it's not great value like its predecessors, but if you can find it on sale, this is still one of the only phones on the market with good performance, a headphone jack, a microSD card slot, and an embedded stylus, if you care about that perk.

Motorola may have cut wireless charging from the 2026 iteration of the Moto G Power, but thanks to some under-the-hood optimization, it performs 
better than its predecessor
. That's a fine trade-off, though it's odd considering the two phones are powered by the same MediaTek Dimensity 6300 chip with 8 GB of RAM. Yes, you'll still notice some slowness here and there—it's a step down from the Moto G Stylus, after all—but it's not a stuttery experience. The other notable hardware upgrades are an improved selfie camera and a slightly larger 5,200-mAh battery capacity.
Battery life is the next best thing about this phone, easily lasting two days with average use. The 6.8-inch LCD screen is bright and sharp enough, and the phone is 
IP68/69-rated
 to survive dips in the pool and inclement weather. Don't expect much from the camera system. If you have steady hands, you can capture passable photos from the 50-MP primary shooter, though it struggles in low light and high-contrast scenes. Introduce any kind of handshake, and you'll get a blurry photo.
If you can deal with those flaws—including the limited software policy of two Android OS upgrades and three years of security updates—the Moto G Power 2026 still checks off a lot of boxes for its $400 price. (Yes, it originally was $300, but Motorola jacked the price by $100.) It has NFC for contactless payments, a microSD card slot to expand on the 128 GB of storage, and, naturally, the coveted headphone jack.

A surprisingly good phone for $250, the 
TCL 60 XE Nxtpaper 5G
's spotlight feature revolves around its matte, paperlike display. While TCL says it lowers blue light from entering your eyes (supposedly to reduce eyestrain and improve sleep quality), there's no substantive evidence that these blockers do much. If you want to jack in, but also unplug from the world, consider the 
Minimal Phone
. This Android handset has a tiny, 4.3-inch e-paper screen and a physical keyboard. It lets you access all of your favorite apps, but since the screen constantly ghosts and isn't suited for video playback, you'll find yourself barely using social media (the cameras are also pretty bad). Asus may have exited the smartphone market, but 
Nubia is here to fill the void
 with its Redmagic gaming phones. The current iteration is the Redmagic 11 Pro—one of the only smartphones on the market with a headphone jack and the latest Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 flagship processor. That means you get some of the best performance around! Other perks include a large 6.85-inch AMOLED screen, a 144-Hz refresh rate, and a massive 7,500-mAh battery. It's designed to play top-tier mobile games, and it does it easily.
The cameras take a back seat, so keep that in mind, and the software experience is lackluster, though the company is now offering five years of updates. Still, if you desperately want a dedicated headphone jack on your smartphone and don't want to compromise on performance, the Redmagic 11 Pro is one of your only options.
You do need to be wary of carrier compatibility—the company lists supported US carriers on the product page, including AT&T, Google Fi, and T-Mobile. There's a 15-day return policy if you want to give it a shot and test if it'll work on your network without problems.
