But Nicole Prause, a neuroscientist studying human sexuality and addiction, says “this is a gross overstatement from the evidence they claim to have.” Prause is not aware of any scientific evidence that masturbation reduces vulnerability to substance abuse, and she was critical of the research methodology.

“Since masturbation can never be placebo—people always know if they masturbated—it is important to use markers that are less subject to bias than self-report,” she says. “They use none. Someone saying ‘I feel focused’ is just that. There are dozens of cognitive tests that could be used to support a self-report. I do not see any.”

However, Prause, who is working on a large laboratory study of male orgasms, says masturbation to orgasm is a potent method of mood management. She adds that the most “dramatic shifts” among her study cohort come from people who report decreased anxiety and increased relaxation after climaxing.

Despite the ubiquity of masturbating, surprisingly little research has tested what happens when healthy men and women do it every day. A 2024 review of 22 studies found masturbation tended to be negatively associated with sexual satisfaction among men, though the findings for women were mixed.

One interpretation is reverse causation: particularly among men, people dissatisfied with their sex lives may masturbate more, possibly increasing their discontent. A 2017 study published in Archives of Sexual Behavior found evidence that masturbation is often compensatory for men—a substitute for when partnered sex is not available—whereas for women it is more complementary, accompanying rather than replacing partnered sex.

Yet masturbation has documented benefits, too, such as stress relief through endorphin and oxytocin release and improved sleep, as well as providing the opportunity for connection with oneself. Public discussions about self-pleasuring, however, remain decidedly uncommon.

“We wanted to fight the taboo and stigma around masturbation and highlight that it's a normal part of a wellness routine, especially if you're single,” says Levin. “It's not some kind of a shameful habit.”
