What Makes a Great Gaming Laptop?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Picking the right gaming laptop can be a delicate balance. The raw power you typically find in gaming desktops takes a lot of energy, which, in a laptop, can drain the battery fast. Similarly, the super-fast refresh rates in gaming monitors can lead to even more power drain. If you plan to keep your laptop plugged in most of the time, you can lean toward more powerful hardware. If you want to play while using battery, balancing power with efficiency should be a priority.
Size is also a factor to consider. When you're gaming at home, you might want the largest, brightest screen you can find, but when you have to carry that screen around, the bulk and weight can be a (quite literal) drag. If you plan to travel with your laptop, you might want to think about how much heft you're willing to lug.
No matter what, you should expect to make some sacrifices that aren't as common with more typical laptops. It's not unusual for a gaming laptop to last less than two hours after you start playing games on it. Unless you have a lot of (usually expensive) power under the hood, you might not be able to play some modern AAA games at their highest level the way you would on a desktop without spending a little time fiddling with the game's graphics settings.
Specs to Look for in a Gaming Laptop
AccordionItemContainerButton
It's easy to get overwhelmed with numbers when you're shopping for a gaming laptop, but there are a few key specs to focus on to get the best bang for your buck. For additional help with all the specifications on offer, check out our 
How to Buy a Gaming Laptop guide
.
RAM:
 Aim for a machine with at least 16 GB of RAM. While 8 GB is workable for those who play less demanding games or have little desire for fancy graphics, you'll be hard-pressed to find a machine with such a small offering these days. The new minimum is effectively 16 GB of RAM, and if you intend to play new AAA games, stepping up to 32 GB is a really good idea. The speed of your RAM can also be a factor. Currently, DDR5 is the best you'll find, but you'll start to see DDR6 replacing it over the next few years.
CPU:
 When you want the fastest processor, newer is usually better. Right now, you'll still find Intel's 14th generation of gaming CPUs out in the wild most frequently, though its latest Core Ultra 200HX Plus series (Arrow Lake Refresh) is increasingly available. Meanwhile, AMD Ryzen is now on the 9000 series. If you want to get into the nitty-gritty, 
CPU benchmarks
 can help you get a sense of how powerful a processor is, but the best indicator is real-world testing (which we do on every laptop we feature).
Discrete GPU:
 As with the central processing units (CPU), there are an overwhelming number of models to keep track of. In most cases, you'll want whatever gaming laptop you buy to have a discrete graphics processing unit (GPU). Some of the latest integrated graphics have changed that advice slightly, but you need to be careful about which model you buy. These days, Nvidia is the primary option, whether that's the previous-gen RTX 40-series GPUs or the latest RTX 50-series. The full range of RTX 50-series graphics cards for laptops includes the RTX 5090, 5080, 5070 Ti, 5070, 5060, and 5050. You'll be able to find a few gaming laptops with AMD GPUs out there, competing with its Radeon 7000 generation. These will often share similar names to their desktop counterparts, but most are technically running the “laptop” version of the GPU, such as the 
RTX 5090
 or 
5080
. All said, real-world testing is the best indicator of performance.
Storage:
 Most laptops now have solid-state drives, which are super-fast and reduce load times considerably compared to the days of hard drives. Games can get massive, so I’d recommend at least a 1-TB internal drive. But if you can swing for a 2-TB option, you'll have a lot more room for multiple AAA games. Fortunately, many gaming laptops let you install more internal storage, so you may be able to expand that later for less money.
Display:
 Most gaming laptop displays are in a 16:10 aspect ratio and start with a resolution of at least 1920 x 1200 with a refresh rate of at least 
120 Hz
. You want something that matches the performance of the laptop's GPU and the types of games you play. Competitive multiplayer gamers will want a higher refresh rate, while single-player gamers will benefit more from higher resolutions. There's even next-gen display technology available, like mini-LED or OLED screens that enable impressive HDR visuals and improved response times.
Which Laptop Brand Is Best for Gaming Laptops?
AccordionItemContainerButton
All major laptop manufacturers have gaming sub-brands these days. Just to name a few, Lenovo has Legion and LOQ, Asus has ROG and TUF Gaming, Acer has Predator and Nitro, and Dell has Alienware. HP previously offered the Victus and Omen brands but began selling under its HyperX sub-brand earlier this year.
Asus has the most full-featured lineup, including high-powered juggernauts and sleekly slim options alike. Asus also offers some of the cheapest models on offer with its TUF brand and some hyper-portable options in the ROG Flow line. MSI is another noteworthy brand, with dozens of models under various names denoting price and performance: Titan, Raider, Stealth, and Crosshair.
Beyond these sub-brands, let's not forget about Razer. The company has earned its reputation for producing the most premium and well-built gaming laptops, often referred to as the MacBook of gaming laptops.
You will, however, find many lesser-known brands of gaming laptops on Amazon. Most are easy to ignore based on specs alone, as they rarely even feature a discrete GPU. But no matter how attractive the price may be, 
avoid these options at all costs
.
How Much Should You Spend on a Gaming Laptop?
AccordionItemContainerButton
For years, I didn't recommend spending less than $1,000 on a gaming laptop. Today, there are some good options, as you can see from our picks above (and from our list of the 
best cheap gaming laptops
). If you find a good discount, there are a couple of great options to consider for less than $1,000. You can find RTX 5050 laptops for cheaper, but be careful, as many of these budget-minded devices make some serious compromises.
On the other side, you can spend close to $5,000 on a top-tier model that sports the latest RTX 5090 and loads of high-end features and specs. It’s also possible to buy a previous-generation gaming laptop with an RTX 40-series GPU onboard if you're strapped for cash. Though I would be wary, as those laptops are already almost three years old at this point.
What Size Gaming Laptop Should You Buy?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Gaming laptops primarily come in three screen sizes: 14-inch, 16-inch, and 18-inch. There are also 15.6-inch displays in an old-school 16:9 aspect ratio, such as the Lenovo LOQ 15 listed above. The difference between these three sizes is pretty dramatic, both in terms of overall footprint and portability. Most people should buy a 16-inch gaming laptop, which tends to be the sweet spot. Many 16-inch gaming laptops offer you the full range of performance, up to the latest CPUs and GPUs (including the RTX 5090), and even come with a wider range of display options, including OLED. Eighteen-inch gaming laptops tend to be gargantuan—not the kind of thing you want to move around much. The larger screen is great if you don't ever intend to connect it to an external display or pack it in a bag to tote around with you.
Meanwhile, 14-inch gaming laptops have become popular over the past few years, and they function best as a hybrid device. They tend to be sleek and minimalist, downplaying the gamer aesthetic for something more professional and buttoned-up. GPU options tend to be more limited at this size, but you can still get some surprisingly solid performance out of these laptops, depending on the configuration. They tend to get better battery life, too.
Is It Worth Getting OLED on a Gaming Laptop?
AccordionItemContainerButton
In a word, yes. OLED has a number of benefits for PC gaming. The extreme contrast of OLED allows the display to excel in HDR performance. HDR really brings games to life, brightening highlights and deepening shadows. It should be mentioned that all current OLED panels being used on gaming laptops are as bright as the 
best OLED gaming monitors
, which can hit over 1,000 nits of peak HDR brightness. Laptops like the Legion 7i Gen 10 can match that brightness, though, which is when you get the most prominent and noticeable HDR effect in games.
OLED is available for both 14-inch and 16-inch gaming laptops but not on 18-inch models just yet. OLED is helpful for more competitive gamers, offering less input lag than on IPS or mini-LED. It's totally worth it. It's increasingly becoming standard on premium gaming laptops, meaning you won't always need to make the hard choice between a faster GPU and a better screen.
