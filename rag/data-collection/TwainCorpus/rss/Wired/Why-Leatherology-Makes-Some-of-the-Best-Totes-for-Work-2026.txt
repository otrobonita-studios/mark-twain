My tote bag
 is more a mobile workstation than a carrier. On any given day, it’s hauling a 16-inch 
laptop
, 
noise-canceling headphones
, 
chargers
, my 
daily planner
, a 
water bottle
, and whatever gadgets I’m testing that week. Plenty of leather totes look beautiful on day one, but far fewer are designed to hold up against the reality of transporting thousands of dollars’ worth of tech every single day.
That’s why Leatherology surprised me.
After months of rotating between the Mia Horizontal Tote, Transit Travel Tote, and Large Zippered Downtown Tote, I found myself thinking less about the leather—though it’s undeniably excellent—and more about how each bag has been intentionally designed to fit specific workflows.
But the first thing you notice, of course, is that leather. Leatherology uses semi-aniline, drum-dyed pebbled leather that is soft and supple, making it feel broken in from day one without being overly delicate.

The hardware is clean, the branding is subtle, and the totes don’t feel ostentatious, which I appreciate. These bags look expensive because the quality of the materials and craftsmanship speak for themselves, not because there’s a flashy logo plastered on them. And if you want something more personal, every model can be monogrammed.
As a tech reviewer, though, I find that aesthetics go only so far. What impressed me most about these bags is their functionality, which is something I can’t say about many luxury bags. The straps remained comfortable even when I loaded the bags with bulky gear. The interiors are well-organized, preventing 
cables
 and accessories from getting lost in a single, cavernous compartment, yet they don’t feel over-engineered. Each bag is sturdy and secure enough to protect your valuables.

Built Better Than It Looks
The 
Mia Horizontal Tote ($330)
 is the most relaxed of the three and has become my go-to bag for the weekend. Its wider silhouette is ideal for farmers market runs, shopping trips, or afternoons working from a coffee shop when I don’t need my entire desk setup. 
My Kindle
, 
headphones
, a sweater, and small essentials all fit comfortably with room to spare. Two interior slip pockets kept accessories within reach, while the removable leather pouch became my designated home for my card holder and lip balm. The magnetic closure allows for easy access during everyday errands, but because it lacks a dedicated laptop sleeve or zippered top, I wouldn’t use it as a work bag for my commutes.
That’s where the 
Transit Tote ($465)
 comes in. This is the bag that feels purpose-built for work trips, where a personal item often doubles as your office. It features two exterior pockets, nine interior pockets, two zippered compartments, and a padded laptop sleeve that fits up to a 
16-inch laptop
. I could separate chargers, travel documents, and 
toiletries
 into designated, well-organized spaces. A hidden trolley sleeve slides over 
rolling luggage
, and the zippered top adds an extra layer of security at airports. Between the short leather handles and longer shoulder straps, it’s also comfortable to carry through terminals without constantly shifting your grip. Plus, it feels much more heavy-duty than the Mia Horizontal, whose straps I sometimes worry might give out when the bag is overpacked. There’s also a foldable bottom gusset with a removable support panel that helps the bag maintain its structured silhouette. During my testing, it fit comfortably under an airline seat, but it’s always worth confirming measurements with your specific aircraft.

Still, as much as I liked the Transit, the 
Large Zippered Downtown Tote ($285)
 became my favorite. It’s the Goldilocks bag of the lineup, the sweet spot between elegance and utility, and better than almost any leather tote I’ve carried. It’s large enough to accommodate my remote work setup without feeling like it might tear, which makes it the bag I reach for most days. Even fully loaded, the structured silhouette holds its shape, giving it a polished appearance that transitions from office meetings to dinner afterward. It adds just enough personality without limiting what I could wear with it, and the recycled polyester and cotton twill lining feels durable enough to withstand years of commuting.
Despite its generous interior, it doesn’t turn into a black hole thanks to two open pockets, a zippered interior pocket, and a removable leather pouch for smaller items. The full-length zipper offers the kind of security I want when carrying a laptop. Leatherology also offers an optional water-resistant insert with insulated and mesh compartments, making the tote even more adaptable for 
parents
, photographers, or anyone who wants additional organization. I don’t have one at the moment, but I would consider purchasing a bag insert to help protect the interior, which could be difficult to clean if it stains.
A Little Extra TLC
At the end of the day, these are also premium leather bags, and while they’re built to be worn, they still require care and maintenance. You’ll want to spot-treat the lining with a baby wipe or a damp cloth to help minimize stains. You should condition the bag every six months or so with an all-natural leather conditioner to help preserve the leather’s texture. When storing the bag, it’s best to stuff it with tissue paper or soft fabric to maintain its shape; for extra protection, I also keep mine in the provided dust bag. And lastly, you don’t want the bag to get wet, so I’d avoid wearing it on rainy days or in extreme weather. If it does get wet, wipe it dry as soon as possible.

Leatherology’s appeal isn’t about chasing trends or projecting status. Instead, it’s about recognizing how modern professionals move through their day. We commute between home and the office, work from coffee shops, catch flights, and expect one bag to transition seamlessly across it all. These totes meet that reality with thoughtful organization and a timeless design built to last. In a zeitgeist where our bags have become extensions of ourselves and our workspaces, that’s exactly what I want.
