It may sound
 hyperbolic to claim the best cordless vacuums can improve your life. But they do hold that power. In addition to providing you the freedom you didn't know you needed, they also leave your home clean and tidy in a matter of minutes with no cord unraveling or heavy vacuum dragging required. These machines are especially handy for me, as I live in a three-story home. Even the prospect of lugging a classic upright vacuum up and down the stairs leaves me unlikely to do the chore. And I can never seem to remember to take my 
robot vacuum
 to different floors to clean the whole house. A stick vacuum, though? Because the form is light and mobile, these vacuums are a breeze to bring to every level and use on my two sets of stairs—and I only expend a tiny bit of sweat in the process.
After testing nearly two dozen stick vacuums—including hard-floor–only models, powerful Dysons, and more—these are the best cordless vacuums I've tried. My new favorite is the 
Shark PowerDetect Speed Cordless Stick Vacuum ($430)
, which not only has impressive power but also bends to make vacuuming under furniture and low surfaces easier. It also has the 
Clean & Empty version ($500)
, which features a self-emptying base station. If you've got room to splurge, you can upgrade to the 
Dyson Gen5Detect ($800)
 for the best power I've seen in a cordless vacuum.

Dyson isn't the only brand with a growing vacuum lineup this year. Shark recently launched the Shark PowerDetect Speed, a new cordless vacuum in its beloved PowerDetect line. The Speed is cheaper than the main model, but it packs a ton of features I like, and it performed excellently in many of my testing rounds.
The PowerDetect Speed is supposed to be a lighter, more agile version of the PowerDetect vacuum. It weighs about a pound less, has 60 minutes of battery life, and has 350 watts of power to the regular PowerDetect's 380 watts. It also comes with the same two attachments as the regular model.
The Speed moves smoothly over carpets and rugs while still picking up a ton of debris; I used this vacuum on my carpeted bedrooms the day after vacuuming with the V16 Piston Animal, and it pulled up tons of dust and cat hair from my rug. The vacuum can bend, too, so you can get underneath furniture without hunching over. The PowerDetect Speed can also determine if you're on carpet or hard floors (or on a messier spot of either type of flooring) and will switch modes accordingly. The 
Clean & Empty version ($500)
 has lights to illuminate dust and will use a different light color to indicate its current mode. Most vacuums with a similar feature only light up for hard floors, but the PowerDetect Speed Clean & Empty uses a blue light to spot dust on hard floors and a brighter headlight on carpet to spot debris you might need to clear out of the way.
The PowerDetect Speed performed better than most Dyson vacuums for vacuuming up sand on a rug, but it did take two or three passes to clean up a large pile of litter I put on my vinyl plank floors. Oh, and it has HEPA filtration.
My only frustration is that the head of the vacuum gets stuck upright frequently when you're using it in handheld mode. It also doesn't have the edge detection that the main model features. If you go for the Clean & Empty version, though, you’ll get a self-emptying charger base and prettier color options. Overall, either the Speed or the regular PowerDetect makes a great investment, but I prefer the lower prices of the former model.

If you're set on getting a Dyson vacuum but you want a more affordable option, the 
Dyson V10 Konical
 (7/10, WIRED Recommends) left me more impressed than I expected. You won’t get the LCD screen that shows battery life or debris level, and the machine is less powerful than other Dyson models. FAQs
What's a Cordless Vacuum?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Cordless vacuums, also known as stick vacuums, are exactly what their name suggests: They don't need a cord to work. Instead, they have a battery you need to charge. Their typical design features a battery and motor at the top, with a long, thin, stick-like body connecting that to the head of the vacuum. They're much lighter than an upright vacuum and have become popular since they're much easier to store and move around the house. As someone who lives in a three-story home, stick models are my favorite type of vacuum, thanks to their comparatively lightweight nature and portability. Stick vacuums can also usually have the stick portion removed to transform into a handheld vacuum, though they're much heavier than a true 
handheld vacuum
 (but the battery life is much better).
How Long Do Cordless Vacuums Last?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Overall, vacuums tend to last around five years, but that depends on the frequency with which you vacuum and the vacuum's build quality. Some cheaper stick vacuums might last only about a year or two, though, according to Eufy, so it's worth investing in a good-quality stick vacuum. For information on the signs your vacuum may need replacing, check out our 
guide to how long vacuums can last
. If you're curious whether you're vacuuming enough, check out our guide to 
how often you should vacuum
.
How Does WIRED Test Cordless Vacuums?
AccordionItemContainerButton
The best way to test a vacuum is to use it like you usually would. So, for a few months, we lived with these cordless vacuums, using them to handle day-to-day messes along with weekly deep cleans on hardwood floors, area rugs, and carpets. We charged them, asked our partners to use them, and even took some to a retail store to clean up antique furniture.
We also performed head-to-head testing, comparing how each model picked up piles of Cheerios and cat litter, noting whether they blew debris around or needed several passes to clean a certain spot. We also took heaps of already matted dust and dirt from inside the vacuum bins to see how easily the vacuums could suck them back up in their thickened state.
