Google may spend
 years 
testing the durability
 of its own hardware, but because accidents happen, you’d still be wise to get a good case for your Pixel 10 phone. Most devices in the 
Pixel 10 line
—the 
Pixel 10, Pixel 10 Pro, Pixel 10 Pro XL

Pixel 10 Pro Fold

flagship phones

No matter which Google Pixel 10 you have, you'll want to protect your device with a case at the very minimum (a screen protector is also a smart buy). This will decrease the odds of an accidental drop ruining your smartphone and preserve the phone’s appearance so it looks great over time. It’s an especially important detail if you plan on trading in the phone for a newer device in the future.

Will Pixel 9 Cases Fit on the Pixel 10 Series?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Sometimes the stars align, and an updated phone model has the identical dimensions, shape, and design as the previous model, allowing you to use an older case. That is, unfortunately, not the case with the Pixel 10 series. Google updated the speakers on this line, so while you may be able to fit some soft Pixel 9 cases over the Pixel 10 phones, you'll run into other issues, namely blocking the speaker port. Pixel 9 cases without magnets will interrupt the Qi2 functionality on the Pixel 10 series. For the best experience, I recommend avoiding a Pixel 9 case altogether, whether it's for the Pixel 10 or Pixel 10 Pro.
Will Pixel 10 Cases Fit on the Pixel 10 Pro?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Most Pixel 10 cases will fit the Pixel 10 Pro, and vice versa. Several retailers market their cases (
even Google
) for both phones. I’ve tested several Pixel 10 Pro cases on the Pixel 10 and have had no issues. The cutouts are all accurate, the button placements are perfect, and the fit is snug. However, Pixel 10 and 10 Pro cases are 
not
 compatible with the Pixel 10 Pro XL.
Will Pixel 10 Cases Fit on the Pixel 10a?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Nope. The Pixel 10a is slightly taller, thicker, and wider than the Pixel 10, and the camera bumps are different, too. These cases are not interchangeable.
What Does ‘Qi2’ Mean for Pixel 10 Cases?
AccordionItemContainerButton
The Pixel 10 phones are the first Android flagships with native 
Qi2 wireless charging
. That means magnets are built into the backs of the phones, just like Apple's 
iPhones and MagSafe
, allowing you to access faster, more efficient wireless charging. Magnets also align the coils perfectly, eliminating any worries about your phone not charging because you didn't place it 
just right
 on a charging pad.
Not every Pixel 10 case has magnets baked in, though most of them do. (Almost all of the cases in this guide feature magnets.) However, I have asked nearly every case maker whether its cases are Qi2-certified by the Wireless Power Consortium (which manages the Qi2 standard), and the answer is almost universally no, which may point to less reliable and efficient charging. For the 
best experience
, make sure to check the product listing to see if it mentions “
Qi2
,” “
Made for Google
,” or “
Pixelsnap compatible
,” which is Google's name for its magnetic accessory system. The latter two certifications at least ensure that the case was subject to a rigorous testing standard.
One neat feature to note: When you place your Pixel 10 on a Qi2 wireless charger, you should see an option to add a screensaver. This isn't a traditional screensaver, though you can totally just set a clock or photo and call it a day. There's also a Home Controls option, which lets you place your Google Home smart devices—like smart lights and smart shades—on the lock screen while the phone is charging for quick access. Head to 
Settings > Display & touch > Screen saver
 to set it up.
Will My Old Wireless Charger Work for the Pixel 10?
AccordionItemContainerButton
While Qi2 is backward compatible with Qi wireless charging, I have extensive evidence that not all Qi wireless chargers are fully compatible with Qi2 phones. The reason may come down to the placement of the wireless charging array on newer devices. By all means, try charging your new Pixel 10 with your preferred case on a wireless charger you already own. If it works, great! It won't be as fast or efficient, but at least it'll work. However, if it 
doesn't
 work, look for a Qi2 charger. You can check out 
several picks here
 (and below).
How I Test Cases
AccordionItemContainerButton
I install every case I receive over the Pixel 10 series and take a look at the fit, whether or not ports or speakers are blocked, and ensure the buttons are clicky and responsive. I also monitor how much the case sticks out from the display—the taller the better, as it'll keep your display off the ground. To test Qi2 accessories, I gauge the strength of the magnetic connection. I also install every screen protector to test the ease of the application process.
I don't do drop tests because I have a limited number of Pixel phones. In my experience, though, a case isn’t a guarantee your phone won’t crack when dropped, regardless of “drop test” claims from a manufacturer. Even so, a case improves your chances of protection, especially if you add a screen protector.

:
 One point to note is that Poetic includes a screen protector that embeds itself into the case, like old-school cases that offered full protection. You can opt not to use it, as the case will work with or without the protector. The Revolution doesn't have any magnets but has a built-in kickstand and a cover that can completely protect your cameras; I find this a little extreme, so I don't care for it. It also, in my humble opinion, looks hideous. The Guardian looks much better, with a thick bumper, raised edges, and a covered port. The buttons are a little stiff, but at least it has built-in magnets for Qi2 (not certified). Finally, the Spartan (for Pixel 10a) has a built-in MagSafe ring stand that lets you use MagSafe accessories, grip your phone securely, and prop it up in kickstand mode. The buttons could be more responsive, but it's an option worth considering if you want a multitool sort of phone case.

 I like many of Peak Design's cases, but not the Gnar case for the

 or the

. The edges of the phone feel way too slippery, and they also push in a little too much into the front screen, which disrupts my screen protector and creates a small air bubble. The lip around the display is also lackluster, and I find it a little too hard to pull out the flap that protects the USB-C charging port. I don't love the two-tone material choice on the back; it feels cheap and dull. The SlimLink square adds an extra layer of security for the Pixelsnap magnetic attachment, but you'll have to pair it with relevant SlimLink docks and mounts to get the most out of it.
