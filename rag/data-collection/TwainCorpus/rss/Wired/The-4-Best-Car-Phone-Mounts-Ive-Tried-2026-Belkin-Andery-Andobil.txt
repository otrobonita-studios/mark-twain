A car phone
 mount is no longer an option—it's a necessity. Nearly every US state restricts cell phone use while driving (we see you, Montana!), and most require full hands-free operation. The best car phone mounts offer an easy view of your phone's screen without blocking your view of the road, hold your phone securely, and keep vibration to a minimum.
I've spent weeks and hundreds of miles testing car phone holders for iPhones and Android alike, on busy freeways and on potholed city roads. What I found is that the best car phone mounts aren't necessarily expensive or name brands: They're just smartly designed.
The best car phone mount for an iPhone is almost always a secure MagSafe mount. The one I keep in my car when I'm not testing other mounts is a sturdy, compact 
Andery suction mount ($36)
 that holds preternaturally fast to both my phone and dash but can also be mounted to a window. My favorite mechanical car phone holder for Android phones is a sturdy, versatile, ball-joint mount from 
Andobil ($30)
 that affixes tightly to a car's dash, windshield, or air vents.

The world is full of bulky phone mounts that block your view of the road, inflexible phone mounts that only allow for horizontal or vertical mounting, weak suction cups, and kinda mid MagSafe attachments. And then there's this innovatively designed, wonderfully compact dash and window mount from Andery.
On flat surfaces, the nano-gel suction cup holds fast like 
Hodor at the door
. The 22-magnet MagSafe ring allows airflow to avoid overheating, but it also holds stronger than most comparable Qi2 magnets. And unlike bulky plastic mounts, the Andery's aluminum-arm, stainless steel ring keeps a low profile and interferes minimally with one's line of sight. In fact, when affixed to my dash, this device doesn't block my view of the road at all. The ring rotates 360 degrees. The two-joint arm can either fold tightly to the mount or extend outward. Mechanical mounts like this one from Andobil—with holders that can be slid in sideways to securely grip the phone—are flexible for a broad range of phones and don't require MagSafe technology. But they're also mostly bulky, making them a less preferred option as a windshield or dashboard mount because this will block your field of vision.
Among all similar devices I've tested, this one from newer Chinese company Andobil is the most compact I've tested when not in use, while still offering a lot of flexibility. The nanogel suction cup on its dash mount is one of the most stable I've tested (almost aggressively stuck on, to be honest). And the extendable arm, while plastic, feels less cheap and breakable than other models. The kit also comes with an air vent mount, whose three-point design makes it among the most stable vent mounts I've tested and allows you to maneuver the phone so it does not block the vent.
This mechanical car phone holder is hard to call aesthetic. It's essentially a pad with a gripper on the side. But the grips extend to accommodate even larger phones and are able to keep the phone in place even during vigorous shaking. (If you're wondering whether I place phones in these phone mounts, and then come at them like they're 
a shake weight?
 I do.) The release button's a little awkwardly placed, and Andobil's marketing about “military standards” is a little silly on a low-cost device made with thermoplastics. But after I watched other suction cups wilt in summer heat, this Andobil's still going strong.
★ A Great, Low-Cost Air Vent Mount:
 If you're mounting only to your air vent, the 
Vanva air vent phone mount ($13)
 is among the cheapest mounts of any kind that I've tested, while ranking among the most secure mechanical mounts. (Actually, I like the mechanical phone holder a bit better than Andobil's.) The lockable clamp affixes easily and securely to most vertical or horizontal-slatted vents—and unlike hook-based vent mounts, it's easy to install and remove. The multi-joint ball arm can be maneuvered so you don't lose airflow from the vent, and the adjustable grips that lock in your phone can be released with a simple button press. This Vanva is a relatively flimsy plastic device, so I don't rule out that it might break someday. But its inexpensive replacement cost makes this an acceptable risk.

How I Test and Select Car Phone Mounts
AccordionItemContainerButton
Car phone mounts are and are not a sensitive technology. Bulky mounts can block your field of view while driving. And it's a genuine safety hazard if your car mount, or your phone, falls on the floor while you're driving down the freeway.
Every phone mount I and other WIRED testers recommend has been road-tested against this possibility, driven for hours on the road, and on long road trips, over sometimes bumpy roads. Often, I kept as many as eight devices at a time glued to my dash, windshield, and vents so that my car looked like a dollar-store communications center. For the most recent round of testing, this included multiple days of relatively humid, 90-degree Fahrenheit heat.
Photograph: Matthew Korfhage
Photograph: Matthew Korfhage
I pulled suction cups off of dashboards and windshields, over and over, to see if suction cups wore out the first time you had to move a device—or if the cups left residue on the windshield. I tested using multiple models of iPhone, a long folding Motorola Razr, and a large-model Nokia, onto each mount to see how it held up—with or without bulky cases.
I clamped each phone in and out repeatedly, to see how easy it is to remove a phone from the mount. I also turned each mount upside down and shook vigorously to see how violent I had to be to shake the phone loose from its mooring.
Where chargers were involved, I tested the charging capacity with ammeters and phone apps, as well as simply by marking how long it took to charge my phone compared to other chargers. I also tested the MagSafe charging speed vs. the speed if I'd just plugged the phone directly into 
my preferred USB car charger
. (The USB always wins, for multiple reasons.)
For the most recent round of testing, I tested a dozen phone mounts during a sticky summer, selecting both best-selling and best-recommended mounts from both well-known brands and a new breed of hyper-specialized brands like Andobil and Andery that have made their name only on car phone mounts. My colleague Simon Hill likewise tested a dozen mounts in a previous round of testing.
Can Android Phones Use MagSafe and Qi2-Compatible Mounts?
AccordionItemContainerButton
MagSafe is a great technology for the more than half of cell phone owners in the US who use Apple products. The best phone mounts, with the fewest compromises, are pretty much all magnetic mounts.
But alas, most makers of Android phones have been more resistant to adding magnetic mounting capability. The 
Google Pixel 10
 is an exception and offers built-in magnets and native Qi2-compatible charging support. Other phone makers, like Samsung, offer MagSafe-compatible cases for newer models like the 
Galaxy S26 series
.
Some makers of MagSafe mounts offer little stick-on devices meant to affix to your non-magnetic phone. Don't bother with these; they don't work that well and don't stay affixed for long. If you'd like the convenience of a magnetic mount, you're going to have to get a MagSafe-compatible case for your phone. And for this, I refer you to WIRED's guide to the 
Best MagSafe Accessories.
Honorable Mentions
MagSafe Mounts
Belkin 15W MagSafe Mount for $80:
 This Belkin MagSafe charger and vent mount is an older device we've recommended previously as a top pick—with a secure magnet pad that offers 15-watt Qi2 charging. The mount positions itself in the air vent and blocks airflow a bit, but on summer days with the air-conditioner running, this positioning can help cool your phone while it charges. It's also a little bit sleek but also a bit on the spendy side for what it offers.
Andobil MagSafe Air Vent Mount for $37:
 This vent-mounted MagSafe device looks like a charger but isn't. The mount's broad, flat surface does stabilize the phone a bit. But in trade, it blocks airflow both to the phone and from the air vent. For these reasons, it's not really my favorite design. But the magnet is strong enough to hold the phone in place, and the vent mount is also stable.
iOttie iTap 3 Air Vent Holder for $16
:
 This compact vent mount from iOttie is low-cost, and the MagSafe mount is strong enough. I can hold this thing upside down and shake it without dislodging my phone. But there's very little range of motion, and the phone can only be mounted where it'll block airflow from the vent. I also tend not to favor mounting mechanisms that use hooks, because it can make the mounts more difficult to remove when needed—though for unusual air vent geometries, the hook might be the only style of vent mount that works.

Vanmass Car Phone Holder for $40:
 Similar to the Andobil I recommend as a top pick, this Vanmass is a ball-socket mount with options for air vent or dashboard/windshield mounting. But while Vanmass advertises “military-grade” tech, whatever this means for a phone mount, the plastic felt a bit flimsy, especially on a vacuum locking mechanism for the suction cup.

This is all well and good when you're driving solo, notes WIRED reviewer Kat Merck, but during longer trips with family, it takes up a much-needed cup holder, and the stem can get in the way. In most cars, you'll have to look a bit too far away from the road to see the screen.

Acer Gadget Car Phone Mount for $28:
 This little car mount from Acer mirrored the design of my top-pick Andery but fell far short on performance. The suction cup pretty much walked off the job after I removed and replaced it once, the magnet isn't as strong, and the mounting arm is shorter and doesn't rotate. There's no particular reason to buy it in a world where the Andery exists.
iOttie iTap 3 Dash and Windshield Mount for $25
:
 MagSafe car phone mounts from iOttie have one chief merit: The magnet is strong and secure, unlikely to dislodge on even the bumpiest roads. But alas, the same cannot be said for the suction cup on this bulky dash and windshield mount, which gave up the ghost on a hot day.
Scosche Magic Mount for $13
:
 This is maybe the most compact, low-cost MagSafe device I tested. Its S-branded design is both unobtrusive and winningly inscrutable: It looks like a talisman Jor-El might have brought back from Krypton. It's also the one with the weakest magnet. My iPhone lurched off at minor provocations.
iOttie Wireless 2 Qi Charging Phone Mount for $50
:
 Don't mistake “Wireless 2 Qi Charger” for a Qi2 charger. A couple of years back, this MagSafe charging mount was a top pick for WIRED, but the charging is now a bit sluggish by modern standards.
