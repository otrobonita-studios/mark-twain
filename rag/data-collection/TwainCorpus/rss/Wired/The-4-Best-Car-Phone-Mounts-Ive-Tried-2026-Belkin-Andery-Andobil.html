A car phone mount is no longer an option—it's a necessity. Nearly every US state restricts cell phone use while driving (we see you, Montana!), and most require full hands-free operation. The best car phone mounts offer an easy view of your phone's screen without blocking your view of the road, hold your phone securely, and keep vibration to a minimum.

I've spent weeks and hundreds of miles testing car phone holders for iPhones and Android alike, on busy freeways and on potholed city roads. What I found is that the best car phone mounts aren't necessarily expensive or name brands: They're just smartly designed.

The best car phone mount for an iPhone is almost always a secure MagSafe mount. The one I keep in my car when I'm not testing other mounts is a sturdy, compact Andery suction mount ($36) that holds preternaturally fast to both my phone and dash but can also be mounted to a window. My favorite mechanical car phone holder for Android phones is a sturdy, versatile, ball-joint mount from Andobil ($30) that affixes tightly to a car's dash, windshield, or air vents.

Here are the best car phone mounts for each car and phone type. See also WIRED's guides to the Best USB Car Chargers, Best Portable Jump Starters, Best iPhone Cases and Accessories, Best MagSafe Accessories, and Best Wireless Phone Chargers.

How I Test and Select Car Phone Mounts

Car phone mounts are and are not a sensitive technology. Bulky mounts can block your field of view while driving. And it's a genuine safety hazard if your car mount, or your phone, falls on the floor while you're driving down the freeway.

Every phone mount I and other WIRED testers recommend has been road-tested against this possibility, driven for hours on the road, and on long road trips, over sometimes bumpy roads. Often, I kept as many as eight devices at a time glued to my dash, windshield, and vents so that my car looked like a dollar-store communications center. For the most recent round of testing, this included multiple days of relatively humid, 90-degree Fahrenheit heat.

I pulled suction cups off of dashboards and windshields, over and over, to see if suction cups wore out the first time you had to move a device—or if the cups left residue on the windshield. I tested using multiple models of iPhone, a long folding Motorola Razr, and a large-model Nokia, onto each mount to see how it held up—with or without bulky cases.

I clamped each phone in and out repeatedly, to see how easy it is to remove a phone from the mount. I also turned each mount upside down and shook vigorously to see how violent I had to be to shake the phone loose from its mooring.

Where chargers were involved, I tested the charging capacity with ammeters and phone apps, as well as simply by marking how long it took to charge my phone compared to other chargers. I also tested the MagSafe charging speed vs. the speed if I'd just plugged the phone directly into my preferred USB car charger. (The USB always wins, for multiple reasons.)

For the most recent round of testing, I tested a dozen phone mounts during a sticky summer, selecting both best-selling and best-recommended mounts from both well-known brands and a new breed of hyper-specialized brands like Andobil and Andery that have made their name only on car phone mounts. My colleague Simon Hill likewise tested a dozen mounts in a previous round of testing.

Can Android Phones Use MagSafe and Qi2-Compatible Mounts?

MagSafe is a great technology for the more than half of cell phone owners in the US who use Apple products. The best phone mounts, with the fewest compromises, are pretty much all magnetic mounts.

But alas, most makers of Android phones have been more resistant to adding magnetic mounting capability. The Google Pixel 10 is an exception and offers built-in magnets and native Qi2-compatible charging support. Other phone makers, like Samsung, offer MagSafe-compatible cases for newer models like the Galaxy S26 series.

Some makers of MagSafe mounts offer little stick-on devices meant to affix to your non-magnetic phone. Don't bother with these; they don't work that well and don't stay affixed for long. If you'd like the convenience of a magnetic mount, you're going to have to get a MagSafe-compatible case for your phone. And for this, I refer you to WIRED's guide to the Best MagSafe Accessories.

Honorable Mentions

MagSafe Mounts

Belkin 15W MagSafe Mount for $80: This Belkin MagSafe charger and vent mount is an older device we've recommended previously as a top pick—with a secure magnet pad that offers 15-watt Qi2 charging. The mount positions itself in the air vent and blocks airflow a bit, but on summer days with the air-conditioner running, this positioning can help cool your phone while it charges. It's also a little bit sleek but also a bit on the spendy side for what it offers.

Andobil MagSafe Air Vent Mount for $37: This vent-mounted MagSafe device looks like a charger but isn't. The mount's broad, flat surface does stabilize the phone a bit. But in trade, it blocks airflow both to the phone and from the air vent. For these reasons, it's not really my favorite design. But the magnet is strong enough to hold the phone in place, and the vent mount is also stable.

iOttie iTap 3 Air Vent Holder for $16: This compact vent mount from iOttie is low-cost, and the MagSafe mount is strong enough. I can hold this thing upside down and shake it without dislodging my phone. But there's very little range of motion, and the phone can only be mounted where it'll block airflow from the vent. I also tend not to favor mounting mechanisms that use hooks, because it can make the mounts more difficult to remove when needed—though for unusual air vent geometries, the hook might be the only style of vent mount that works.

Non-MagSafe (Mechanical) Mounts

iOttie One Touch Dash and Windshield Mount for $28: This dash and windshield mount from iOttie was a favorite of WIRED tester Simon Hill when he tested it two years ago. But there's been a design update and some reports of durability issues, so until we test the newest model, I'm leaving this in the honorable mentions.

Vanmass Car Phone Holder for $40: Similar to the Andobil I recommend as a top pick, this Vanmass is a ball-socket mount with options for air vent or dashboard/windshield mounting. But it's a bit bulkier than the Andobil, and the air vent mount is a hook-based mount—which I tend to find more difficult to remove. The suction cup on the dash/windshield mount is ridiculously strong and is unllikely to dislodge even on hot days—and didn't leave residue when I removed it. But while Vanmass advertises “military-grade” tech, whatever this means for a phone mount, the plastic felt a bit flimsy, especially on a vacuum locking mechanism for the suction cup.

Bokilino Car Cup Mount for $16: This is a different sort of phone mount than most I tested: It affixes to a standard cup holder and holds up your phone like a liberty torch from atop a bendable gooseneck stem. This is all well and good when you're driving solo, notes WIRED reviewer Kat Merck, but during longer trips with family, it takes up a much-needed cup holder, and the stem can get in the way. In most cars, you'll have to look a bit too far away from the road to see the screen.

Not Recommended

Acer Gadget Car Phone Mount for $28: This little car mount from Acer mirrored the design of my top-pick Andery but fell far short on performance. The suction cup pretty much walked off the job after I removed and replaced it once, the magnet isn't as strong, and the mounting arm is shorter and doesn't rotate. There's no particular reason to buy it in a world where the Andery exists.

iOttie iTap 3 Dash and Windshield Mount for $25: MagSafe car phone mounts from iOttie have one chief merit: The magnet is strong and secure, unlikely to dislodge on even the bumpiest roads. But alas, the same cannot be said for the suction cup on this bulky dash and windshield mount, which gave up the ghost on a hot day.

Scosche Magic Mount for $13: This is maybe the most compact, low-cost MagSafe device I tested. Its S-branded design is both unobtrusive and winningly inscrutable: It looks like a talisman Jor-El might have brought back from Krypton. It's also the one with the weakest magnet. My iPhone lurched off at minor provocations.

iOttie Wireless 2 Qi Charging Phone Mount for $50: Don't mistake “Wireless 2 Qi Charger” for a Qi2 charger. A couple of years back, this MagSafe charging mount was a top pick for WIRED, but the charging is now a bit sluggish by modern standards.
