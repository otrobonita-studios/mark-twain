Google’s

Google's Tensor G4 chipset powers this phone, the same as in 2024's flagship 
Pixel 9

The 
Pixel 11
 isn't as big a step up from the 
Pixel 10
 as it was from the 
Pixel 9
. It brings iterative improvements, including a slightly better camera, a more efficient chipset, and slightly faster 
Qi2 25W wireless charging
, alongside fresh, AI-infused software. This is the Pixel for anyone who wants to enjoy Google's unique features but doesn't want to spend $1,099 on the 
Pixel 11 Pro

It has the same screen size as the Pixel 10 and 10a and 3,000 nits of peak brightness, making it easy to read the display in broad daylight. The Tensor G6 chipset offers a tasty mix of upgrades, promising 20 percent gains in power efficiency, 25 percent faster browsing, and 15 percent faster app loading. It broadly matches the Pixel 11 Pro models on performance, and you'll find it silky smooth for everything but the most demanding games (hardcore gamers might prefer something else).
Google has finally upgraded the base model to 256 GB of storage, though that means the starting price is $100 higher than last year's Pixel 10. You can also snag it with 512 GB for an extra $120, and both use speedy UFS 4, enabling faster app load times, quicker file saves, better gaming performance, and even better battery life.
The Pixel 11's battery will easily last you a full day, but much depends on what you're doing; if you're playing 
Wuthering Waves
 for more than an hour, that'll sap juice fast. That's why the Qi2 charging system is so great. You can easily attach a 
magnetic battery pack
 to the back of the phone and wirelessly charge it—faster and more efficiently than traditional Qi chargers. Just make sure the power bank is Qi2.

Like the Pixel 10, the Pixel 11 has a triple-camera system, but Google upgraded the main camera's image sensor. The Pixel 11 is the way to go if you can spend a little more. How Long Is Your Pixel Supported?
AccordionItemContainerButton
The Pixel 11 series, Pixel 10 series, Pixel 9 series, and Pixel 8 series will get 7 years (from their release date) of Android OS upgrades and security updates. The Pixel 7 series, Pixel 6 series, and original Pixel Fold will receive five years of Android OS upgrades and security updates from their launch dates. The Pixel 5 series and older are no longer supported. Google has a 
page you can check
 to see exactly what month your Pixel phone will stop receiving updates. Security updates reduce the risk of malware, keep your phone bug-free, and make it more secure. Android version updates introduce new features—cosmetic and functional—that improve the OS as a whole.
Pixel phones are also the first to receive Android updates, so as soon as Google releases a new version, like 
Android 17
, you can download it immediately by heading to 
Settings
 > 
System
 > 
System Update
 > 
Check for Update
. You can also test beta versions of Android, but you'll need to 
enroll your device
. (Make sure to 
back up your phone
 first!) Pixels also get the 
feature drops
 Google releases every few months, including the ones introduced in newer Pixels that trickle down to older models, plus completely new features for the latest devices.
What Is Qi2?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Qi2 is the 
next-gen wireless charging standard
, following the Qi wireless protocol. It uses magnets embedded in the backs of smartphones to align perfectly with magnets in Qi2 wireless chargers, delivering faster, more efficient charging with less heat. It's practically the same as 
Apple's MagSafe technology
 (Apple helped develop the Qi2 standard), and all MagSafe iPhones now support Qi2, starting from the iPhone 12. However, not all phones that claim Qi2 functionality are actually Qi2 phones. Samsung's 
Galaxy S26 series
 is notably 
Qi2 Ready
, meaning the phones don't have magnets inside, and the only way they can achieve Qi2's charging speeds and efficiency is with a magnetic case.
You don't have to worry about that with the Pixel 11 phones. They're all proper Qi2 devices with magnets inside. Google calls it Pixelsnap, and that's also the name it's using for its suite of accessories. The Pixel 10 Pro XL was the first and only phone from Google to support the newer Qi2.2 25W standard, which bumps charging speed from 15 to 25 watts, but all four Pixel 11 series phones do. That means they'll wireless charge the fastest, provided you use a 
compatible charger
.
If you have an older Qi wireless charger lying around, it may not work well with the new phones. Qi2 is backward compatible, but we've run into issues with older chargers not recharging devices consistently. You'll probably need to upgrade to a Qi2 charger. If you get a case, make sure it's Pixelsnap- or Qi2-ready with magnets inside.
What About Older Pixel Phones?
AccordionItemContainerButton
Several Pixel phones are worth considering if you don't want any of the above choices. The 
Pixel 9
 and 
Pixel 9 Pro XL
 are available through official channels and third-party retailers (until supplies last). But if they're not heavily discounted, you may as well put that money toward the Pixel 10 or Pixel 11 series.
We do not recommend you buy any older Pixel devices. Several Pixel A-series phones have been put under a battery performance program to mitigate the risk of overheating or swollen batteries. These software updates have crippled battery life on those phones, and Google had to offer customers battery replacements or Google Store credit for a new Pixel phone. These potential issues mean it's just not worth the risk of buying a 
Pixel 7a
 or 
Pixel 6a
. We also don't think the 
Pixel 8a
 is worthwhile anymore as the price hasn't come down much; you're better off buying the Pixel 9a or Pixel 10a.
Pixel A-Series Battery Issues
AccordionItemContainerButton
In 2025, some 
Google Pixel 4a
 and 
Pixel 6a
 owners received mandatory updates that intentionally degrade battery performance to prevent overheating. Their phone may have been working fine before, but Google issued these updates as a preventative measure, even if not every device was affected. Similarly, some 
Pixel 7a devices
 may experience battery swelling.
If your Pixel 7a has been affected, you can get a free battery replacement through 
Google's registration page
. Unfortunately, the programs for the Pixel 4a and 6a have been discontinued.
These issues seem to have only affected some batteries in the A-series phones, suggesting it's likely a supplier issue. What about the Pixel 9a? Here's a statement a Google spokesperson provided to WIRED: “Google continuously improves Pixel testing based on user feedback and thoroughly investigates reported incidents for all components, including batteries. With Pixel 9a, we delivered the largest and longest-lasting battery of any A-series yet. We’ve been encouraged by its reception so far and are confident in the phone’s longevity and durability.”
As for the Pixel 10a, Google continues to include the Battery Health Assistance feature by default, which will lower the battery's maximum charge after you hit a certain number of charge cycles. It's designed to protect the battery over several years. We'll be monitoring it over the next few years.
What Does Pixel Care+ Include?
AccordionItemContainerButton
When you purchase a Pixel from Google, you'll see the option to add 
Pixel Care+ for protection
. It's only available in the US through the Google Store and Google Fi. The monthly option lasts up to 60 months, whereas the fixed pricing is for a two-year term. Here's what you get.
Pixel Care+ starts at $10 per month or $199 for a two-year plan
 for the latest Pixel 11 series and includes unlimited accidental damage protection, $0 screen and battery repairs, replacements that ship in a day, and priority support from Pixel experts. That pricing is for the Pixel 11, and it goes up for the pricier devices. For example, the Pixel 11 Pro Fold will cost $19 per month, or $319 for the two-year term. The cheapest Pixel Care+ option is for the Pixel 8a, 9a, or 10a is $6 per month or $119 for two years.
Pixel Care+ with Loss and Theft
 
starts at $11 per month or $219
 for the Pixel 11 and includes the same protections as mentioned above but includes two claims of loss and theft every 12 months. Weirdly, Loss and Theft coverage is not available in New York state.

Daily Hub (Pixel 10):
 Similar to Samsung's Now Brief feature, this Daily Hub is nestled in Google Discover, but will also show up in the At a Glance widget a few times a day. It'll give you upcoming calendar events, reminders powered by Magic Cue, and even suggest new activities to try based on your Google Search history.
Now Playing:
 Ever hear a song at the coffee shop and wonder who it's by? Now Playing uses on-device machine learning to figure out what music is playing in the background, and it displays the artist and title on the lock screen (or in your notifications). It doesn't require an internet connection, so there's no data shared between you and Google. This isn't turned on by default, so head to 
Settings
 > 
Sound and Vibration
 > 
Now Playing
 and toggle it on. You can even turn on Now Playing History in case you forgot to write down an artist's name when you saw it earlier. Google added a button you can press in case Now Playing doesn't automatically identify a song, sorta like Shazam.
