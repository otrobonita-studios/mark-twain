The Best Car Vacuums

Any Vacuum Can Be a Car Vacuum

There are plenty of vacuums marketed as being designed for cars, but ultimately, just about any vacuum will do. Even a clunky upright vacuum works if you've got an outlet in your garage and the will to wheel it around to both sides and the trunk of your car.

Two styles are specifically easier for car cleaning: handheld vacuums, which are small and great for quick cleans, or cordless vacuums, which are super portable and have a removable extension wand that turns them into a hand vacuum. True handheld vacuums are cheaper and lighter, but usually come with limited battery life (I'm talking 10 to 20 minutes) and small dustbins. A cordless vacuum will be more powerful and have a longer battery life, and often comes with more accessories (but not always). The downside is it will be heavier and a bigger investment.

What to Look for (And What to Skip) in a Car Vacuum

Here's what I'd make sure a vacuum has if I'm buying it to clean my car:

  • A crevice tool. There are a lot of attachments you'll see out there. I find the crevice tool is frequently the most useful for cleaning a car, especially as the mom of a 4-year-old who covers the car in pieces of snacks and an alarming amount of sand.
  • Hair tools if you’re a pet owner. I see a lot of names for the small motorized head that helps pull up pet hair. Dyson calls it a hair screw tool, for example, while Bissell calls it a FurFinder attachment. This isn't critical equipment, since car upholstery isn't deep, but these attachments are helpful if your car's carpets and seats are covered in pet hair.
  • Extension abilities. If it's not a small handheld you can easily reach into tough or far-away spots with, make sure it has a long hose or wand you can use to help get every inch of your car clean.
  • Battery life. Some of these handheld vacuums don't even have 20 minutes of battery life, but if you're cleaning a sedan or small SUV, that's plenty of time to vacuum the seats and floors. If you have a large vehicle, or multiple vehicles you're vacuuming, you'll want something with a bigger battery.

What I'd worry less about:

  • Suction power. Your car upholstery isn't deep, so you don't need major suction to get the dirt and debris out of the floor and cushions like you would a carpeted home.
  • Brush-style accessories. I really wasn't impressed with these when cleaning my car. They didn't help pick up sand or car dust nearly as much as I thought they would on any of my car's surfaces. If you're worried about dust, try a vacuum with an air duster, like the Scosche Scovac 4-in-1 below.

FAQs

Should You Keep Car Vacuums in Your Car?

I wouldn't keep a handheld vacuum in your car for a long period of time. The heat and outdoor temperatures your car could sit in, both in a garage or out in the elements, are more extreme than these vacuums are made to handle, and you risk damaging the battery. Plus, there isn't a way to keep it charged in the car, so you might go to use it and find it completely out of battery.

Where Should You Store a Car Vacuum?

I'd keep all vacuums, car or not, indoors in your home. They should also be safe in a well-insulated garage, especially one with accessible outlets you can use to charge your vacuum. But if your garage gets extremely hot or cold, I'd say it's safer to store the vacuum inside your house.
