The new 
$225 Pebble Time 2 smartwatch
 feels like a toy, and we mean that in the best sense of the word. The refreshed version of the indie smartwatch brand, rebooted by its original founder, feels casual, fun, and playful, — not like a mini-computer on your wrist. Its quirky watch faces and simple apps and games give it a sort of hacker energy, while its physical buttons, e-paper screen, and long battery life provide its retro vibes.

The first thing you’ll notice about the new Pebble Time 2 is that the device looks nothing like an Apple Watch. Where the Apple Watch has an OLED touchscreen display, the Pebble opts for a color e-paper screen. Where the Apple Watch comes in a range of band types, from sporty straps to high-end metals and leather, the Pebble Time 2 has a silicone watch band, making it feel more like a Swatch rival than a fancy smartwatch. And instead of picking between different aluminum finishes, as with the Apple Watch, the Pebble Time 2 
offers a choice of colors
: blue, red, silver, or black wrapped around its stainless steel frame of silver or black.

Pebble Time 2 colors.
Image Credits:
Core Devices

The watch’s 64-color e-paper screen is always on but can be backlit (via an RGB LED) with a flick of your wrist or via taps. Like almost everything on the watch, you can customize this, too. (I set mine to light up with a single tap instead of two.)

This display is more muted compared with an Apple Watch’s Retina display, but it’s easy to see in bright sunlight, and the trade-off here means the battery can last a long time: about 21 days on a charge, depending on use, with a charger included in the box.

But where Pebble really differentiates itself from other smartwatches is its software and, especially, its community. The community is really the reason the watch still exists at all — after the company behind the original Pebble shut down.

Pebble’s comeback

Pebble had first begun as a Kickstarter project before ultimately making its way to Google, by way of a Fitbit acquisition. Before its exit, the company sold 2 million watches, generating $230 million in sales. Even so, its founder, Eric Migicovsky (who has since co-founded the messaging app Beeper), openly shared that Pebble’s financials 
weren’t able to sustain the business
, which forced the sale.

Fitbit kept the watch’s servers and app store alive until mid-2018. After that, the job was taken over by 
Pebble’s own user community
, an unusual show of brand loyalty that effectively kept the product on life support for free.

Image Credits:
Core Devices

Then, in 2025, Migicovsky, with the help of some ex-Pebblers and Fitbit folks at Google, convinced the tech giant to 
open source
 the original Pebble operating system. This made it possible for him to take another crack at building 
Pebble watches
 under his new company, 
Core Devices
.

Core Devices has since shipped a limited-edition Pebble 2 Duo watch and has now sold more than 25,000 of 
its Pebble Time 2 watches
 across 93 countries. Its third device, 
the Pebble Round 2
, is expected to begin shipping in September.

While Migicovsky hopes the do-over will give him a chance to correct the mistakes of the past, what hasn’t been left behind is Pebble’s spirit. 

Watch faces! So many watch faces!

Still very much sustained by its hacker community, the device already ships with over 2,120 watch faces and apps built by Pebble developers, including over 500 new ones from a 
spring developer contest
. This is where things start to get fun. Since the first thing many people do is customize their watch face, this large, whimsical selection showcases the community’s playful vibes.

Pebble Time 2.
Image Credits:
Core Devices

Using the Pebble companion mobile app, you can choose from simple analog or digital displays, or you can pick from those featuring silly characters, like cartoon dogs, cats, or axolotls; various backgrounds and scenes; pixelated retro displays; giant, brightly colored numbers; text shown styled in creative fonts, like those from comic books or sci-fi; and more.

You can set the screen to be a favorite meme, like Nyan Cat, or to a watch face with characters from classic video games like Mario, Kirby, Pokémon, and more. (Don’t ask about the licensing — just enjoy it while it lasts.)

Image Credits:
TechCrunch

Apps and games

Despite a much smaller user base than the Apple Watch, the Pebble app store is full of creative and often niche applications across categories like health and fitness, games, tools and utilities, remotes, and others. There are many useful tools here — timers, Spotify controls, weather apps, to-do lists, calculators, navigation aids, stock trackers, a Strava activity tracker, medication reminders, health apps, calendars, and many fun, often retro-inspired, games.

Pebble app store.
Image Credits:
Core Devices

What’s incredible about the selection is that many of these were created by individuals who simply wanted an app for a specific purpose, so they built it themselves. That could be an app for tracking a local train schedule, or their tennis score, their Claude AI usage, when to apply more sunscreen, or a thousand other things that would never make sense for an indie developer to build as a business.

There are also newer AI-powered apps for those who want access to a chatbot or agent on their wrist.  

There are many playful apps, too, like the 
PebGoBrrrr
 app that tells time by vibrating the watch, or one where you can 
whack a mole
 for fun. (I don’t know, why not?). Among the 
most popular apps
 is the self-described “pointless” 
Pixel Miner
 game, where your little character mines for treasure. Another popular creation offers a 
FitCat virtual pet 
and activity tracker.

Pixel Miner game.

There’s even 
an app you can use
 to make other apps.

If there’s any complaint to be made here, it’s that there’s almost too much to explore. The Pebble app store could benefit from more search and discovery tools, similar to Apple’s App Store — subcategories, lists, editorial selections, or personalized recommendations. You could spend hours, weeks, or months browsing the app store and still find new things.

Image Credits:
TechCrunch

All the basics, too

However, even if you used the Pebble Time 2 without installing extra apps, the watch is still very functional. It mirrors the notifications sent to your phone, which you can individually toggle on and off in the Pebble companion app. Plus, you can control your music playing on your phone, set alarms and timers, and more. While the app doesn’t market itself as a fitness tracker, it does offer some basic functions, like tracking steps, sleep, and heart rate.

Image Credits:
TechCrunch

The watch also offers a classic Pebble feature known as the timeline, accessed by the down button on the right side. This lets you scroll through your day, including past and upcoming calendar events, reminders, weather, and 
pins
 added by other apps. It’s an at-a-glance look at what you have coming up next, which is handy.

Image Credits:
TechCrunch

The Pebble Time 2’s buttons are intuitive to use. The watch comes 
with four buttons
, more than you’ll find on the Apple Watch, but they make sense. The top button is the up button, the bottom one is down, and the middle is select. The button on the left side of the watch is back. What’s more, you can 
customize any button to launch a favorite app
 with a tap or long press. (By default, a long press on the back button launches Quiet Time, which silences your notifications.)

Further specs are 
available on Migicovsky’s blog
. Note that the watch has some water resistance — 30 m / 3 ATM — which means it can handle everyday water exposure like rain or handwashing, but don’t go swimming or diving with it.

Being less connected: a perk or a problem?

Where the watch falls short is mainly in areas the company can’t control, like its 
compatibility with iOS
. Apple’s rules restrict the watch from being able to do many things, like taking actions on notifications, responding to texts, or 
making various iOS apps work with the watch
. That means even basic features, like your daily alarms, won’t sync over automatically to the Pebble — you’ll have to set them up directly on the watch itself. 

But there’s a silver lining to this. Because Pebble is more limited, it doesn’t become another thing you have to pay attention to and engage with. You can stay connected without feeling an obligation to immediately respond or take some sort of action. Though it took some adjustment, I’ll admit, after a while I found it easier to simply glance at the watch and look away without feeling the tug to do something. 

What’s worth watching here is where this go next. The company ships new firmware every few weeks and updates its companion mobile app. It’s been adding new APIs and other improvements after launch, and still plans to add features in the future, like enabling the second mic for noise cancellation.
