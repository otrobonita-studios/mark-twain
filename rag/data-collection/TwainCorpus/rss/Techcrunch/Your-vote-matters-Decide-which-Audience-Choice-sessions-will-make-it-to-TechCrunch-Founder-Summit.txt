How to Build Events Strategy Before You Can Afford a Full-Time Events Team 

Alexandra Ames: Founder and Chief Event Strategist, 
Blueprint Lab

Early-stage founders approach events one of two ways: say yes to every opportunity, or avoid them entirely because the ROI feels impossible to justify. Neither is smart. Events can build your audience and credibility before product-market fit but only if you’re strategic about which ones deserve your time and budget. We’ll discuss how to think strategically when resources are tight, including how to maximize ROI from founder events like this one. You’ll leave with frameworks for prioritizing stage-appropriate event investments, tactics for proving value without complex tools, and clarity on when events make sense and when they don’t.

How to Build a “Made in the USA” Hardware Company and Actually Scale It 

Hannan Happi: CEO and Co-Founder, 
Exowatt

Domestic manufacturing is back in the spotlight, but building hardware in the U.S. is still widely seen as too expensive to scale. This session explores how founders can design hardware companies that make “Made in the USA” economically viable from day one. We’ll break down supply chain strategy, cost structure, factory partnerships, and how policy tailwinds like the IRA and CHIPS Act change the equation. Attendees will leave with a practical framework for evaluating when domestic manufacturing can become a competitive advantage — not just a branding decision.

Build Something We Don’t Have a Word for Yet

Andy Tang: Partner, 
Draper Associates

Pitches that change industries rarely fit existing categories — and most investors will pass on them for exactly that reason. This talk explores why the most fundable startups are often the hardest ones to describe, and what that means for how you build and position yours. Founders will leave with a clearer sense of the difference between a market insight and a market-creating idea, and what it actually takes to get a pattern-matching investor to bet on something genuinely new — before the rest of the world has a name for it.

GraphRAG in Action: Stop Hallucinations with Knowledge Graphs

Shishir Tewar: Head of Enterprise Data Engineering, 
Procore Technologies

Are messy data sources causing your LLMs to hallucinate? Relational databases struggle to bridge the semantic gap for AI. Learn to build a production-ready GraphRAG pipeline that turns chaotic data into actionable insights. Actionable takeaways: Build an offline pipeline to resolve disjointed datasets into “golden records.” Translate flat records into an interconnected Neo4j knowledge graph. Deploy a secure, local LLM (Ollama) to translate natural language into validated Cypher queries. Give your AI the foundational data structure it needs!

The Enterprise AI Playbook: How to Close L’Oreal and Vodafone Before You Raise a Series A 

Merve Kitapci: Managing Partner and CGO, 
ADIN.AI

Most founders chase product-market fit before landing enterprise clients. We did the opposite. This roundtable covers the exact motion for closing global enterprise accounts at pre-Series A: how to structure the first conversation with a Fortune 500 CMO; why agency partnerships accelerate enterprise sales; how to price with no brand recognition; and what zero organic churn means for your fundraising story. Attendees leave with a repeatable enterprise sales framework, an early-stage AI pricing model, and the three things that make a CMO say yes before legal sees the contract.

Lessons from 20 Years of Bootstrapping an Open Source Business to $50M+

Peter Farkas: CEO, 
Percona

Building a successful open source company without venture capital is rare — scaling it past $50 million in revenue is even rarer. This talk shares two decades of lessons from growing a commercial open source business from zero to global scale. We’ll cover product-market fit, monetizing open source while keeping the core free, building distributed teams before remote work was mainstream, and creating a repeatable go-to-market engine without outside funding. We’ll also discuss failures, hard lessons, and how to compete against well-funded alternatives while staying bootstrapped. Attendees will leave with practical strategies for scaling sustainably.

Small but Mighty: How AI Lets Lean Teams Outship the Giants

Linden Tibbets: CEO and Co-Founder, 
IFTTT

The playbook has flipped, and we have lived it firsthand. A five-person team can now outbuild, outship, and outmaneuver companies with 500 engineers. This roundtable gets into the tactical reality of running a lean, AI-powered team: which tools are actually worth your time, how to build workflows that compound instead of bloat, and where most teams are burning cycles on AI that goes nowhere. Walk away knowing how to audit your current stack for speed gaps, which AI-driven growth motions are converting right now, and how to use your small size as an advantage, not an excuse.

The Demo Trap: Why Impressive AI Doesn’t Always Build Real Companies

Jeff Mahler: Co-Founder and CTO, 
Ambi Robotics

In today’s AI boom, impressive demos can generate headlines, funding, and early excitement, but they rarely prove a real business by themselves. This session explores the gap between prototype performance and real-world deployment, and why many promising AI startups stall before reaching scale. Founders and investors will learn how to evaluate whether AI systems are truly ready for production, the operational and reliability metrics that matter most, and how to build companies that can move beyond hype to durable, real-world impact.

From CentOS to Rocky Linux: Building Movements That Outlast You 

Gregory Kurtzer: CEO and Founder, 
CIQ

Greg Kurtzer helped create CentOS, one of the world’s most widely used operating systems, then founded CIQ and launched Rocky Linux when Red Hat discontinued CentOS. In this session, he shares hard-won lessons from building open source movements and a startup from scratch: why founders must leave ego at the door, then navigate the transition from hiring versatile generalists early, then moving to a team of specialists as you scale. He explains the mistake he almost made by dismissing the value of culture and core values, and what it really takes to build something durable.

The Healthcare AI Companies That Will Actually Matter 

Vikram Chaudhery, PhD: CEO and Founder, 
Genoa Ventures

Most AI healthcare demos look incredible and ship nothing. The companies that will matter in 2026 aren’t the ones with the best models. They’re the ones that get decisions to clinicians in an actionable window and change what doctors actually do for the patient in real clinical workflows. This roundtable digs into what separates investable healthcare AI from expensive science fairs, where agentic systems are creating real value beyond copilots, what founders consistently get wrong before their Series A, and the bar that anyone funding, founding, or joining a healthcare AI company should apply.

How Technical AI Founders Get Into YC, a16z Speedrun, and Raise from Top-Tier VCs

George Bandarian: Founder and General Partner, 
Untapped Ventures

The best technical founders building in AI and robotics aren’t failing because of their product — they’re failing because they don’t understand what top investors are actually looking for. In this roundtable, early-stage technical founders will work through what YC, a16z speedrun, and top-tier VCs evaluate beyond the deck: founder-market fit signals, why most technical founders need a business counterpart earlier than they think, how to position an autonomous economy company for a lead investor, and what the fundraising process actually looks like before you have traction.
