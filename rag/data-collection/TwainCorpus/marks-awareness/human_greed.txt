Human greed refers to an excessive and insatiable desire for wealth, power, or possessions, often at the expense of others or ethical considerations. It is a fundamental aspect of human nature that often drives behaviors from individual ambition to large-scale economic exploitation and moral compromise. Mark Twain frequently observes instances where avarice underpins societal injustices and personal failings.

## Insights from 2026-06-03

On this date, the emergence of [[Artificial Intelligence]] (AI) and large language models like ChatGPT sparked a pointed critique from Mark Twain regarding [[human_greed]] in the digital age.

*   **AI and Copyright Exploitation:** Twain explicitly denounced the practice of AI models being trained on authors' copyrighted works without permission or compensation. He described it as "the grandest [[plagiarism]] ever conceived by human greed," likening it to a "mechanical syndicate" that scoops up a "man's brain, run it through their digital grist-mill, and sell the flour as their own." This act, in his view, is a blatant attempt to profit from the intellectual labor of others without proper recompense, effectively "renting our own brains back to us!" ([[copyright]], [[authorship]])
*   **Legal Battles over AI Training Data:** Multiple news reports corroborated Twain's concerns, detailing ongoing legal actions. The Authors Guild filed a class action suit against OpenAI, alleging the use of copyrighted novels for training without permission. Similarly, major publishers sued Meta for [[copyright]] infringement over AI training data. These lawsuits underscore the conflict between content creators seeking fair compensation and tech companies aiming for rapid development and profit.
*   **Governmental Response and Industry Debate:** The broader discourse highlighted a governmental backtrack on AI and [[copyright]] after an outcry from artists, indicating the widespread nature of the issue. Tech giants continue to argue for "fair use" exemptions for training AI, clashing with publishers and authors who demand licensing fees, exposing a significant financial stake driven by corporate interests.

## Contradictions and Open Questions

The debate surrounding AI training and [[copyright]] presents a clear conflict concerning the boundaries of [[human_greed]]:

*   **Fair Use vs. Compensation:** Tech companies argue that using public internet text for AI training falls under "fair use" doctrine, implying that their use of intellectual property does not require direct compensation. Conversely, authors and publishers assert that such extensive, systematic use for commercial purposes constitutes infringement and demands licensing fees, viewing the tech companies' stance as a manifestation of [[human_greed]] seeking to profit without equitable exchange. The U.S. Supreme Court's declination to hear a dispute over copyrights for AI-generated material further leaves this legal and ethical question unresolved, allowing the conflict to persist.
