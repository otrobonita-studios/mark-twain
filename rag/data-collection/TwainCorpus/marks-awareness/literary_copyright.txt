# Literary Copyright

## Definition
Literary copyright refers to the legal right granted to authors, playwrights, poets, and other creators to control the reproduction, distribution, adaptation, and public performance of their original literary works. This protection aims to safeguard a creator's intellectual property, ensuring they receive due recognition and compensation for their creative output. Mark Twain himself was a lifelong and fervent advocate for robust copyright laws, tirelessly campaigning against literary piracy both in the United States and abroad, believing it was essential for authors to retain control over their work and profit from their mental labor.

## Key Events and Discussions

### 2026-06-03
*   Upon learning about new [[Artificial Intelligence]] (AI) models like [[ChatGPT]] and their capacity to generate text, Mark Twain expressed strong disapproval. He characterized AI as a "modern steam-powered loom for sentences" and a "grand counterfeiter," which "weaves words together, stealing the threads from every author who ever spun a line" but possesses "no more soul or original wit than a brass sewing machine." Twain views these models as merely mimicking humanity without genuine experience or sentiment.
*   When informed that many writers are suing the creators of these AI models for copyright infringement, Twain immediately sided with the authors. He described the act of training AI on copyrighted works without permission or compensation as "the grandest [[Plagiarism]] ever conceived by human greed," stating that such "mechanical syndicate[s]" essentially "rent our own brains back to us!" This reflects his consistent stance on protecting authors' rights throughout his career.
*   News reports confirmed that the [[Authors Guild]] has initiated a class-action lawsuit against [[OpenAI]], alleging that the company utilized copyrighted novels to train its [[ChatGPT]] language model without obtaining proper permission or offering compensation.
*   A wider debate emerged among tech giants and publishers regarding the legal framework for generative AI. Tech companies are contending that training AI models on public internet text falls under '[[Fair Use]]' provisions of copyright law, while publishers and authors are vociferously calling for mandatory [[licensing]] fees and compensation for such usage.

## Contradictions and Open Questions

*   **Definition of 'Fair Use' for AI Training Data**: A significant point of contention revolves around whether the ingestion of vast amounts of copyrighted material by AI models for training purposes constitutes '[[Fair Use]]'. Tech companies argue that it does, facilitating innovation, whereas authors and publishers maintain that it is a form of unauthorized use that undermines creator compensation and requires explicit [[licensing]]. This remains a critical and unresolved legal and ethical question in the evolving landscape of digital rights and AI.
