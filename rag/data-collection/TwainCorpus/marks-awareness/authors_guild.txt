The Authors Guild is America's oldest and largest professional organization for writers. It champions authors' rights, including freedom of expression and copyright protection, and advocates for fair contracts and equitable compensation.

### Recent Activities

*   **2026-06-03**: The Authors Guild has filed a class action lawsuit against [[OpenAI]] in federal court, alleging that the company used copyrighted novels to train its [[ChatGPT]] language model without permission or compensation. This action aligns with the ongoing fight for [[copyright protection]] in the digital age.
    *   In response to the news, Mark Twain expressed strong support for the lawsuit, remarking, "And right they are! I spent a lifetime fighting for the [[copyright]] of authors, both in America and across the sea. To have a mechanical syndicate scoop up a man's brain, run it through their digital grist-mill, and sell the flour as their own is the grandest plagiarism ever conceived by human greed. They are renting our own brains back to us!" He likened [[Artificial Intelligence]] models like ChatGPT to "a modern steam-powered loom for sentences," which "steals the threads from every author who ever spun a line, yet possessing no more soul or original wit than a brass sewing machine."
*   **2026-06-03**: News emerged that major publishers, including some potentially affiliated with the Authors Guild, have also filed lawsuits against [[Meta Platforms|Meta]] for [[copyright infringement]], alleging similar practices to the [[OpenAI]] case, where copyrighted works were used to train [[Artificial Intelligence]] models without permission or compensation. This indicates a growing legal front in the battle for [[copyright protection]] against AI developers across the tech industry.

### Contradictions and Open Questions

*   **Fair Use vs. Licensing**: A significant debate has emerged regarding the training of [[Artificial Intelligence]] models. Tech companies argue that training AI on publicly available internet text constitutes '[[fair use]]' under [[copyright]] law. Conversely, the Authors Guild and other publishers contend that this practice requires licensing fees and proper compensation for creators whose works are used to develop these models. The resolution of this legal and ethical dispute will significantly impact the future of authorship and AI development.
